5 Signature Cold Coffees — The Barista's Training Guide

By Nigel A. Thomas • Hospitality & F&B Training • October 2026

Cold coffee has developed into a major beverage category for modern cafés, hotels, resorts and food-and-beverage operations. Guests expect cold coffee to be properly chilled, balanced, attractive and consistent. The difference between an average iced beverage and a signature drink is usually not complicated equipment; it is disciplined preparation.

This guide presents five signature cold beverages from the accompanying Nigel A. Thomas training poster. The recipes cover espresso, cold brew, Americano, matcha, caramel, honey, chocolate, cream and cold foam. All five are served in a 16 oz cup, over ice.

Cold Coffee Service Principles

Consistency starts with measurement. Coffee strength, milk quantity, sweetener, ice and toppings all influence the final drink. When a beverage is sold as a signature item, the guest should receive the same experience regardless of which trained barista prepares it.

Keep milk and cold mixers chilled. Use clean ice and a dedicated ice scoop. Measure syrups and sauces rather than relying on free-pouring when recipe consistency is required. Keep glassware and equipment clean and ready before the shift begins.

Barista Training Rule

Measure first. Build consistently. Check the presentation. Serve immediately.

Recipe cards and training posters are most useful when they become part of the daily station routine rather than something used only during induction.

The 5 Signature Cold Coffees

01. Iced Honey Caramel Mocha

9 g coffee (for espresso) • 30 ml espresso (1 shot) • 180 ml milk (6 oz) • 15 ml caramel syrup (0.5 oz) • 15 ml honey (0.5 oz) • 15 ml chocolate (0.5 oz) • ice as needed

Specification: 16 oz (480 ml) cup • served cold, 4–8°C • espresso at 9 bar (130 psi) • rich, smooth, refreshing.

Method: Pull the espresso from 9 g of coffee. Combine the caramel, honey and chocolate with the espresso and milk. Build over ice and mix so the flavours are evenly distributed. Serve immediately.

Making tips: Use fresh espresso (9 g of coffee for 30 ml), steam the milk properly (65–70°C), use a good-quality chocolate sauce, and add the caramel and honey in the right order for the best taste. Serve immediately.

Note for trainers: the sheet gives a steaming temperature of 65–70°C for the milk, yet the finished drink is served cold at 4–8°C. Decide your house method (for example, whether the steamed milk is chilled before it meets the ice) and write it on the station recipe card.

02. Honey Caramel Cold Brew Cold Foam

Medium-fine ground coffee (for cold brew) • 80 ml cold brew coffee • 15 ml honey • 15 ml caramel • ice as needed • cold foam as needed

Specification: 16 oz cup • served cold • no machine pressure required • sweet, smooth, refreshing.

Method: Brew the cold brew for 12–24 hours for a smooth taste. Make the cold foam from fresh milk, blended until creamy. Add ice to the cup before pouring the cold brew so the drink stays cool and fresh, add the honey and caramel, and finish with a smooth layer of cold foam.

The combination of cold brew, honey, caramel and cold foam gives the drink a smooth dessert-style character while retaining the flavour of the coffee.

Making tips: Use fresh coffee beans for better flavour, brew the cold brew for 12–24 hours, use good-quality honey and caramel, and make the cold foam creamy and stable enough to provide a clear finishing layer.

03. Iced Chocolate Cream Protein Americano

9 g ground coffee, medium-fine (for espresso) • 30 ml espresso (25–30 sec) • chocolate syrup as needed, mixed with milk • chocolate cream topping (milk cream) • ice plenty

Specification: 16 oz plastic cup • served cold, 4–10°C • espresso at 9–10 bar • rich, creamy, energizing.

Method: Pull 30 ml of espresso from 9 g of medium-fine coffee in 25–30 seconds. Mix the chocolate syrup with milk to make the chocolate cream. Build the espresso over plenty of ice and top with the chocolate cream.

Making tips: Use fresh coffee beans, grind medium-fine for a smooth espresso, pull the shot in 25–30 seconds, use plenty of ice for a refreshing taste, and top with chocolate cream. Adjust only according to the coffee and equipment standard used by the operation.

Note for trainers: the recipe name refers to a protein boost, but the sheet lists no protein ingredient, and no water for the Americano. Confirm your house build before costing the drink or making any protein claim to guests.

04. Strawberry Iced Matcha Tiramisu Cream

2 g matcha powder • 160 ml milk • 15 ml strawberry purée / syrup • 15 ml tiramisu syrup • milk cream made into tiramisu cream, topping • ice as needed

Specification: 16 oz cup • served cold • no machine pressure • fresh, creamy, irresistible.

Method: Prepare the matcha smoothly. Layer the strawberry element in the cup, add the milk and matcha with ice, and finish with the tiramisu cream. Shake well and add ice before serving, and keep the presentation clean and attractive.

The drink combines earthy matcha with strawberry sweetness and a dessert-style tiramisu finish.

Making tips: Use high-quality matcha powder for better taste and colour, make the tiramisu cream smooth and fluffy, layer the strawberry syrup well for a beautiful look, and serve cold.

05. Vanilla Oreo Matcha Iced Cold Cream

2 g matcha powder • 30 ml espresso • 160 ml milk • 15 ml vanilla syrup • crushed Oreo biscuits • milk cold foam (iced), made into vanilla Oreo cream

Specification: 16 oz cup • served cold • no machine pressure • fresh, creamy, refreshing.

Method: Prepare the matcha and espresso components. Combine with chilled milk and vanilla syrup. Shake well, add ice, and finish with the milk cold foam as vanilla Oreo cream. Complete with crushed Oreo.

This dessert-style cold beverage combines matcha, espresso, vanilla and cookie flavours. Presentation is part of the guest experience, so keep the topping clean and consistent.

Making tips: Use high-quality matcha powder, make the vanilla cream smooth and fluffy, crush the Oreo biscuits well for a rich flavour, and shake well before adding ice and serving.

Training the Bar Team

The five recipes demonstrate different beverage-building techniques. The Iced Honey Caramel Mocha focuses on espresso, milk and dessert flavours. The cold brew recipe teaches batch consistency and cold foam finishing. The Chocolate Cream Protein Americano reinforces espresso extraction and iced service. The two matcha beverages introduce modern layered presentation and the combination of coffee, tea, dairy and dessert-style toppings.

A useful training exercise is to have a trainee prepare the same beverage three times using measured ingredients. Compare sweetness, coffee strength, temperature, appearance and topping. The purpose is not simply to memorise the recipe but to understand the variables that affect consistency.

Cold Coffee Mise en Place

Before service, the bar should have coffee beans, espresso equipment, cold brew, milk, matcha, syrups, chocolate, honey, cream, cold foam ingredients, ice, cups and garnishes ready. Every item should have a defined place at the workstation.

Cold beverages are particularly sensitive to warm ingredients and poor ice management. Chilled mixers, sufficient ice and fast service help preserve the intended temperature and texture.

Quality Control Checklist

Before service: Check coffee, espresso equipment, cold brew, milk, matcha, syrups, chocolate, cream, ice and cups.

During service: Measure ingredients, follow the recipe sequence, maintain extraction standards and keep cold ingredients chilled.

Before handover: Check cup cleanliness, temperature, topping and presentation.

After service: Clean equipment and replenish the station for the next service period.
