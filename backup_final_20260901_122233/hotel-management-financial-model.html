Hotel Management Financial Model

1. Costs

Direct operating costs are closely connected to the products and services a hotel sells.

  • Food cost
  • Beverage cost
  • Amenities cost
  • Room supplies
  • Housekeeping cost

2. Expenses

Operating expenses support the property and its people but must be actively controlled.

  • Staff salaries
  • Electricity & water
  • Maintenance & repairs
  • Marketing & OTA costs
  • Taxes & licences

3. Earnings

Revenue comes from multiple departments and channels, not only guestrooms.

  • Room revenue
  • F&B revenue
  • Events & banquets
  • Other services
  • Walk-in customers

How Hotel Managers Should Read This Model

The circular model is deliberately simple: costs reduce gross contribution, expenses consume operating resources, and earnings are created by the hotel's revenue-producing activities. The management task is not simply to cut spending. It is to control unnecessary cost, manage productive expenditure and grow the right revenue streams without damaging guest experience.

Management principle: The strongest hotel operators do not manage the P&L only at month-end. They watch operational drivers every day Ã¢â‚¬â€ occupancy, room rate, departmental revenue, food and beverage cost, labour productivity, utilities, maintenance, purchasing, waste and channel commissions.

Cost Control

Food, beverage, amenities, room supplies and housekeeping consumables should be controlled through purchasing standards, approved suppliers, receiving checks, storage discipline, portion control, inventory counts, recipe costing and waste monitoring. A low cost percentage is not automatically good management if it is achieved by reducing quality or guest service.

Expense Management

Payroll, utilities, repairs, marketing, OTA commissions, taxes and licences require budgeting and regular variance review. Managers should compare actual results with budget and forecast, investigate material variances, and distinguish between unavoidable expenditure and spending that can be prevented, negotiated or timed more effectively.

Earnings & Revenue Management

Room revenue is only one part of a hotel's earning potential. F&B, banquets, events, ancillary services and walk-in business can materially change the property's financial result. Revenue management therefore needs to look at rate, volume, mix, contribution and distribution cost rather than revenue alone.

Five Daily Questions for a Hotel Manager

  1. Are today's revenues ahead of, on, or below forecast?
  2. Which department is creating the largest positive or negative variance?
  3. Are labour hours aligned with business volume?
  4. Are purchasing, waste and inventory controls protecting departmental margins?
  5. What action taken today will improve tomorrow's operating result?

The Goal: Profitability Without Losing the Guest

Hotel financial management is a balance. Cutting service, staffing or maintenance too aggressively can create future costs through complaints, poor reviews, equipment failure, staff turnover and lost repeat business. The objective is profitable operational excellence: the right people, the right standards, the right controls and the right revenue strategy working together.
