7 Types of Coffee & How They're Made

Know Your Coffee. Serve It Better. — A professional breakdown of the seven coffee drinks every hospitality team should be able to make, explain and recommend with confidence.

By Nigel A. Thomas
Hospitality Executive & Corporate Trainer

Great Coffee Doesn't Happen by Chance — It Happens by Choice

Every great coffee drink starts from the same base: a shot of espresso. What separates one drink from another is how that espresso is built on — with hot water, steamed milk, milk foam, or chocolate. Understanding these building blocks is what allows a server, barista, or F&B associate to move confidently between a guest's simple black coffee order and a detailed request for a "dry cappuccino" or "extra hot flat white."

In hotels, restaurants, cafés and cruise line operations, coffee service has become as much a part of the guest experience as the meal itself. Guests increasingly expect staff to know the difference between a Macchiato and a Mocha, to recommend a drink based on taste preference, and to prepare it correctly and consistently every time.

Why Coffee Knowledge Matters in Hospitality

Coffee is one of the highest-frequency touchpoints in food and beverage service — guests order it at breakfast, after lunch, mid-afternoon, and after dinner. A server who can answer "what's the difference between a latte and a cappuccino?" without hesitation builds guest confidence immediately, the same way strong wine or tea knowledge does.

Hospitality Insight

Good coffee, good day. The way a team handles something as simple as a coffee order signals the standard of the entire operation to the guest. Consistent recipes, correct milk texture, and accurate terminology are small details that build big trust.

1. Espresso

A small, strong coffee made by forcing hot water through finely ground coffee under pressure. It is the base for many other coffee drinks and the starting point for everything else on this list.

Key Characteristic

Strong & Bold.

Service Tip

Espresso should be served immediately after extraction — it loses its crema and flavour quickly. Always serve in a pre-warmed demitasse cup.

2. Americano

Espresso combined with hot water. It has a lighter taste and larger volume than a straight espresso, making it closer in strength to drip coffee while keeping the depth of flavour that only espresso can provide.

Key Characteristic

Smooth & Light.

Service Tip

Add the hot water to the espresso, not the espresso to the water, to preserve the crema on top of the cup.

3. Cappuccino

Made with espresso, steamed milk and milk foam, usually served in a smaller cup. The classic ratio is roughly one-third espresso, one-third steamed milk and one-third foam.

Key Characteristic

Creamy & Frothy.

Service Tip

A guest asking for a "dry" cappuccino wants more foam and less steamed milk; a "wet" cappuccino wants less foam and more steamed milk. Knowing this distinction is a mark of a well-trained barista.

4. Café Latte

Made with espresso and plenty of steamed milk, topped with a thin layer of foam. It has a smooth, milky taste and is generally the mildest of the milk- based espresso drinks.

Key Characteristic

Smooth & Milky.

Service Tip

Latte art belongs here — the higher milk-to-foam ratio and thin foam layer make it the ideal canvas for a heart, rosetta or tulip pattern.

5. Mocha

A combination of espresso and chocolate with steamed milk, often topped with whipped cream or a chocolate drizzle. It sits at the intersection of coffee and dessert.

Key Characteristic

Sweet & Chocolatey.

Service Tip

Use quality cocoa or chocolate syrup and add it directly to the espresso before the milk, so it dissolves fully rather than settling at the bottom of the cup.

6. Macchiato

Espresso with a small amount of steamed milk or milk foam. The word "macchiato" means "marked" or "stained" in Italian, referring to the small mark of milk on top of the espresso.

Key Characteristic

Strong & Balanced.

Service Tip

Don't confuse a traditional Macchiato with a "Caramel Macchiato" from speciality coffee chains — the traditional version is a small, strong drink, not a large milk-forward one.

7. Flat White

Made with espresso and finely textured steamed milk. It generally has less foam than a cappuccino and a stronger coffee flavour than a latte, thanks to a higher ratio of espresso to milk.

Key Characteristic

Rich & Smooth.

Service Tip

Micro-foam is the key skill here — the milk should be velvety and almost silky, with virtually no visible large bubbles, poured to create a thin, glossy top.

Knowing the difference between these coffees helps you serve guests better and create the perfect coffee experience — good coffee, good day, happy guests, better service, passion in every cup.

Coffee Service in Hotels & Restaurants

Coffee service standards are just as important as the recipe itself. Consistency, presentation and correct equipment use are what separate an average cup from a memorable one.

Professional Service Standards

  • Always use freshly ground coffee wherever possible.
  • Steam milk to the correct texture for each drink — microfoam for flat whites, denser foam for cappuccinos.
  • Pre-warm cups before pouring.
  • Serve espresso-based drinks promptly after preparation.
  • Offer sugar, alternative milks and syrups separately.
  • Clean and purge the steam wand after every use.
  • Know your machine's dose and extraction time for consistency.

Hotel Manager's Note

Coffee is a repeat-visit driver. Guests remember a well-made cappuccino long after they've forgotten what they ordered for breakfast. Training every team member on these seven drinks pays for itself in guest satisfaction scores.

Interesting Coffee Facts

  • Espresso is the base of every drink on this list — nothing here exists without it.
  • "Macchiato" is Italian for "stained" or "marked."
  • A true Cappuccino is traditionally never ordered after 11 a.m. in Italy.
  • The Flat White originated in Australia and New Zealand.
  • Latte art is possible because of the ratio of steamed milk to foam, not the type of milk used.
  • An Americano was originally created by adding hot water to espresso to approximate the strength of drip coffee.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is the base of every coffee drink on this list?

Espresso. Every drink here is built by adding hot water, steamed milk, milk foam, or chocolate to an espresso shot.

2. What's the real difference between a latte and a cappuccino?

A latte has more steamed milk and a thin layer of foam, making it smoother and milkier. A cappuccino has roughly equal parts espresso, steamed milk and foam, giving it a stronger, frothier character.

3. Is a Flat White the same as a small latte?

Not quite. A Flat White uses a higher ratio of espresso to milk and features finely textured microfoam, giving it a stronger, richer flavour than a latte of the same size.

4. What does "dry" or "wet" mean when ordering a cappuccino?

"Dry" means more foam and less steamed milk. "Wet" means less foam and more steamed milk.

5. Which of these drinks has no milk at all?

Espresso and Americano are both served without milk.

6. Which drink is best for someone who wants a chocolate coffee?

Mocha — it combines espresso, chocolate and steamed milk, and is often finished with whipped cream.

Conclusion

Every one of these seven drinks starts from the same shot of espresso. Once a hospitality professional understands how water, milk, foam and chocolate transform that base, ordering, recommending and preparing coffee becomes second nature.

Great coffee doesn't happen by chance — it happens by choice: the choice to grind fresh, steam correctly, serve promptly, and know the difference between every cup that leaves the bar.

Professional Takeaway

Coffee knowledge is guest-facing knowledge. A team that can confidently explain and prepare all seven of these drinks delivers a noticeably better guest experience — one cup at a time.
