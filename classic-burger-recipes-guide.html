16 Classic Burger Recipes The Complete Chef & F&B Guide

Sixteen burgers, exact quantities for one burger each, safe cooking temperatures and a printable poster. A practical reference for chefs, line cooks and F&B managers.

By Nigel A. ThomasOctober 2026~8 min readRecipe Reference

A burger looks like the simplest item on a menu, which is exactly why it exposes a kitchen so quickly. Patty weight drifts, buns arrive soggy, cheese goes on too early, and the same burger tastes different on every shift. Guests notice, and so does the food cost report.

This guide gives you 16 classic burger recipes with exact ingredient quantities for one burger each, so they scale cleanly from a single order to a full banquet. Every recipe has a short method and the safe cooking temperature that matters on a working line. The poster at the bottom of the page puts all 16 on one sheet you can print for the kitchen.

"A good burger is not a secret recipe. It is the same weights, the same temperatures and the same build, every single time."

01 Why Standard Recipes Matter

Standardised quantities are the foundation of consistent quality and accurate costing. When every beef burger starts at 150 g, every cook plates the same product, and your costing sheet reflects what actually leaves the pass. The same discipline underpins good menu engineering, which we cover in Types of Menus in F&B Service and Food Cost Basics.

The 16 burgers below share a small core of ingredients: bun, lettuce, tomato, mayonnaise and a handful of proteins. That overlap is deliberate. It lets a kitchen offer real variety on the menu while keeping purchasing and mise en place manageable.

06 Kitchen Standards for Every Burger

  • Cook ground beef and lamb patties to 160°F (71°C) and poultry patties to 165°F (74°C); cook fish to 145°F (63°C)
  • Check a sample patty with a probe thermometer each shift and record the result
  • Toast every bun so it holds up against sauces and juices
  • Melt cheese on the patty in the final minute, not earlier
  • Keep raw meat, poultry and fish apart from ready-to-eat toppings, with dedicated boards and utensils
  • Weigh patties before cooking, and keep frying oil fresh and at the right temperature for crumbed items
  • Flag allergens at order: wheat (buns, flour, breadcrumbs), egg, milk, fish and mustard appear across these recipes

07 Frequently Asked Questions

How many burgers should a menu carry?

Most venues do well with four to six: a beef burger, a cheeseburger, a chicken burger, a fish or premium special and a vegetarian option. Add specials from the remaining recipes when ingredients are in season or overstocked.

Can these recipes be scaled for banquets?

Yes. Every quantity is for one burger, so multiply by the cover count and add a small allowance for waste and testing. Always run a small trial batch before a large event.

What is the biggest cause of a dry burger?

Overcooking and overworking the meat. Handle the mix lightly, shape patties evenly, rest them briefly after cooking and rely on a thermometer rather than guesswork.
