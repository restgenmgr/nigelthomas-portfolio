10 Best Resume Writing Tools & Platforms (2026 Guide)

A practical comparison of the top resume builders — features, pricing, ATS compatibility, and which tool suits which job seeker.

📅 April 2025 ⏱ 12 min read 🏷 Career Development & Tools

Your resume is still the single most important document in a job search. It is read — or rejected — in seconds by both automated systems and human recruiters. Writing one from scratch is hard. Writing one that passes Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) and still looks good to a hiring manager is harder.

That’s why millions of professionals use online resume builders. These platforms guide you through the writing process, suggest industry-specific phrasing, format the layout, and export an ATS-friendly file in minutes. But not all resume tools are equal. Some prioritise design, others prioritise ATS compatibility, and pricing models vary wildly — from free forever to subscription traps.

In this guide, I’ve reviewed the 10 best resume writing platforms in 2026, based on usability, template quality, ATS performance, pricing transparency, and real-world usefulness for hospitality, F&B, hotel management, and corporate professionals. Whether you’re a seasoned General Manager or a fresh hospitality graduate, one of these tools will get you to a polished resume faster.

Quick summary: Canva is best for visual design. Resume Genius and Zety are best for guided writing and ATS optimisation. Novoresume and Enhancv are best for modern, standout designs. VisualCV is best for hosting an online portfolio. Resume.io and My Perfect Resume are best for speed and ease of use. SlashCV is best if you want free templates without commitment.

1. Canva — Best for Visual Design

Canva Free + Pro

🔗 canva.com

The design powerhouse that quietly became one of the best resume builders.

Canva started as a graphic design platform and grew into a full productivity suite. Its resume builder offers hundreds of templates — from minimal single-column layouts to highly visual two-column designs. Every element is drag-and-drop, so you can adjust spacing, colours, fonts, and icons without any design experience.

  • Pros: Free tier is genuinely usable; extensive template library; excellent typography and colour control; exports to PDF, PNG, and JPG; integrates with LinkedIn and other Canva tools.
  • Cons: Some templates use multi-column layouts that confuse ATS; no built-in content suggestions; PDF export can create text-as-image issues unless you choose the right setting.
  • Best for: Creative roles, hospitality managers wanting a modern-looking CV, and anyone who values design freedom over guided writing.

Verdict: If you know what to write and just need a beautiful layout, Canva is hard to beat. Pair it with an ATS check before applying.

2. Resume Genius — Best for Guided Writing

Resume Genius Freemium

🔗 resumegenius.com

A guided, step-by-step builder with strong content suggestions.

Resume Genius is one of the oldest and most trusted names in online resume writing. It walks you through each section — contact info, summary, experience, education, skills — and suggests pre-written bullet points based on your job title. The templates are clean, professional, and built with ATS in mind.

  • Pros: Large library of job-specific bullet points; ATS-friendly templates; easy PDF export; mobile-friendly editor; strong free tier with one basic template.
  • Cons: Advanced templates and unlimited downloads require a paid plan; not the most creative-looking layouts; upsell prompts can feel aggressive.
  • Best for: Job seekers who struggle with what to write, not just how to format it.

Verdict: A solid all-rounder for hospitality and corporate roles. If you want help phrasing your achievements, this is a strong starting point.

3. Zety — Best for ATS Optimisation

Zety Freemium

🔗 zety.com

Built around beating the bots — and explaining how they work.

Zety combines a resume builder with an extensive library of career advice articles. Its templates are deliberately simple and ATS-friendly, and the editor flags potentially problematic formatting. Zety also offers a matching cover letter builder, so you can produce a coordinated application pack quickly.

  • Pros: Strong ATS focus; extensive content suggestions; integrated cover letter builder; excellent blog and tutorials; clean PDF exports.
  • Cons: Free tier only allows one resume and one download; subscription auto-renews unless cancelled; some users find the paywall pushy.
  • Best for: Anyone applying to large companies that use automated screening — hotels, cruise lines, corporate hospitality groups.

Verdict: If you’re applying through big job portals, Zety’s ATS-first design philosophy pays off.

4. Novoresume — Best for Modern Standout Designs

Novoresume Freemium

🔗 novoresume.com

Minimalist, confident layouts that still respect ATS rules.

Novoresume is known for its distinctive, modern templates — think clean typography, subtle colour accents, and generous whitespace. Unlike many design-heavy builders, Novoresume’s templates are tested for ATS compatibility, so you don’t have to choose between beauty and machine readability.

  • Pros: Beautiful, modern templates; ATS-tested; section-level content tips; easy colour and font customisation; cover letter builder included.
  • Cons: Free plan limited to one resume and basic templates; premium is on the pricier side; fewer templates than Canva.
  • Best for: Mid-career professionals who want a polished look without sacrificing ATS performance.

Verdict: A strong option if you want your CV to stand out visually while still passing through screening software.

5. Resume.com — Best Free Option

Resume.com Free Forever

🔗 resume.com

A truly free resume builder — no credit card, no watermark.

Resume.com is one of the few platforms that offers a genuinely free, unlimited-use resume builder. You can create, edit, and download as many resumes as you want without paying. Templates are basic but clean, and the editor is simple enough for anyone to use.

  • Pros: Completely free; unlimited resumes and downloads; no watermark; simple, distraction-free editor; ATS-friendly formats.
  • Cons: Limited template variety; fewer content suggestions than paid tools; basic design compared to Canva or Novoresume.
  • Best for: Students, entry-level candidates, and anyone who wants a functional resume without paying.

Verdict: The best zero-cost option on this list. Perfect for graduates or first-time job seekers.

6. VisualCV — Best for Online Portfolios

VisualCV Freemium

🔗 visualcv.com

Resume + personal website + analytics in one platform.

VisualCV goes beyond the PDF. It lets you publish your resume as a live web page with a custom URL — great for linking from LinkedIn or an email signature. The platform also shows you who viewed your CV and when, which is unique among resume builders.

  • Pros: Hosted online resume with analytics; custom URL; multiple templates; ATS-friendly PDF exports; useful for freelancers and consultants.
  • Cons: Free tier limits the number of resumes and hides analytics; premium required for full features.
  • Best for: Hospitality consultants, trainers, and professionals who want a digital presence beyond a static PDF.

Verdict: Ideal if you need both a downloadable resume and a shareable online version.

7. Enhancv — Best for Personality-Driven Resumes

Enhancv Freemium

🔗 enhancv.com

Turns a dry CV into a story about you.

Enhancv focuses on making resumes memorable. It encourages you to add personality-driven sections — like key achievements, passions, and “life philosophy” — alongside traditional content. The templates are visually distinctive but still readable by ATS.

  • Pros: Unique, personality-focused templates; content suggestions that go beyond generic phrases; ATS-friendly exports; excellent for career changers.
  • Cons: Free tier limited to one resume; some templates might be too creative for conservative industries; subscription required for full features.
  • Best for: Candidates applying to modern, culture-driven companies — boutique hotels, lifestyle brands, startups.

Verdict: A good fit if you want to stand out in a crowded applicant pool without sacrificing clarity.

8. Resume.io — Best for Speed and Simplicity

Resume.io Freemium

🔗 resume.io

Create a solid resume in under 15 minutes.

Resume.io is designed for speed. The editor is intuitive, the templates are clean and ATS-friendly, and the guided prompts help you fill sections quickly. It’s particularly good at generating bullet points based on your job title, using a database of pre-written achievements.

  • Pros: Fast, intuitive editor; strong content suggestions; clean, ATS-compatible templates; cover letter builder included.
  • Cons: Free tier only allows one resume with limited features; exports are watermarked unless you upgrade.
  • Best for: Job seekers who need a good resume now and don’t want to overthink the process.

Verdict: A reliable, quick tool that gets you from zero to a finished resume in a single sitting.

9. My Perfect Resume — Best for All-in-One Job Search

My Perfect Resume Freemium

🔗 myperfectresume.com

Resume builder + job search tools in one dashboard.

My Perfect Resume combines resume writing with job search features — job alerts, application tracking, and a matching cover letter builder. It’s a comprehensive platform aimed at candidates who want everything in one place rather than juggling multiple tools.

  • Pros: Integrated job search and tracking; wide template library; ATS-friendly exports; cover letter builder; content suggestions by industry.
  • Cons: Free trial leads to paid subscription; some users report difficulty cancelling; interface can feel busy.
  • Best for: Candidates applying to many jobs at once who want a single dashboard for everything.

Verdict: Useful if you want resume writing plus ongoing job-search tooling, but watch the subscription terms.

10. SlashCV — Best Free Templates Without Commitment

SlashCV Free

🔗 slashcv.com

Simple, free resume templates — no signup required.

SlashCV is a lightweight alternative to the subscription-heavy platforms. It offers a set of clean, professional templates you can edit directly in the browser and download without creating an account. There are no aggressive upsells and no hidden paywalls.

  • Pros: Free to use; no signup required; simple editor; clean ATS-friendly templates; quick export.
  • Cons: Limited template variety; fewer content suggestions; no cover letter builder; basic features compared to paid tools.
  • Best for: Candidates who just want a clean template fast and don’t need guided writing help.

Verdict: A minimalist option that respects your time — and your wallet.

Which Tool Should You Choose?

There is no single “best” resume builder — it depends on your priorities. Here’s a quick decision guide:

  • You want full design control: Canva
  • You need help writing content: Resume Genius or Zety
  • You want a modern, ATS-safe design: Novoresume or Enhancv
  • You don’t want to pay anything: Resume.com or SlashCV
  • You want an online portfolio too: VisualCV
  • You need a resume in 15 minutes: Resume.io
  • You’re applying to dozens of jobs: My Perfect Resume

Final Advice for Hospitality & F&B Professionals

In hospitality, your resume is often read by a General Manager or HR Director who has seen thousands of CVs. What makes yours stand out is not the design — it’s the specific, measurable achievements you list. “Reduced food cost by 4% across two outlets” beats “responsible for cost control” every time.

Use any of the tools above to get the formatting right, then spend your real energy on the content. Pair your resume with a tailored cover letter, and always run the final PDF through an ATS checker before applying.

Your resume is not a static document — it evolves with every role, every achievement, and every career move. Treat it as a living asset. Update it at least twice a year, even when you’re not job hunting. When opportunity knocks, you’ll be ready.
