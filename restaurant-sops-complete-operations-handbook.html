Restaurant SOP Manual

The Complete Operations Handbook for Restaurant Managers, Supervisors, Team Leaders and Hospitality Professionals.

Introduction

Every successful restaurant operates on one foundation: consistency. Guests return because they receive the same quality of food, service and hospitality every time they visit. That consistency does not happen by chance—it is built through well-designed Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs).

Whether you manage a fine dining restaurant, café, quick-service outlet, resort restaurant or multi-unit operation, documented SOPs help every employee understand exactly what is expected. They reduce errors, improve efficiency, strengthen food safety, increase guest satisfaction and support profitable business growth.

This handbook has been created for restaurant owners, general managers, operations managers, supervisors, chefs and hospitality students who want to build professional restaurant systems. It combines practical operational knowledge with industry best practices to help create consistent, measurable and high-performing restaurant operations.

What is a Restaurant SOP?

A Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) is a documented set of step-by-step instructions that explains how a task should be completed. SOPs create consistency by ensuring every employee follows the same process regardless of shift, experience level or location.

In the restaurant industry, SOPs cover every aspect of daily operations—from opening the front door in the morning to completing end-of-day cash reconciliation. They define service standards, food preparation procedures, hygiene requirements, cleaning schedules, inventory management, guest interaction protocols and emergency response plans.

Why SOPs Matter

Restaurants operate in a fast-paced environment where hundreds of decisions are made every day. Without clearly documented procedures, service quality becomes inconsistent, food safety risks increase and operational costs rise. Well-written SOPs reduce training time, improve accountability, simplify supervision and provide employees with confidence in their daily responsibilities.

Front of House (FOH) Standard Operating Procedures

The Front of House (FOH) team creates the restaurant's first and last impression. Professional greeting, efficient seating, accurate communication and attentive service are critical to guest satisfaction. Every interaction should reflect the restaurant's service philosophy and brand standards.

Primary FOH Objectives
  • Create a positive first impression.
  • Welcome every guest professionally.
  • Maintain accurate reservations.
  • Reduce guest waiting time.
  • Communicate effectively with the kitchen.
  • Deliver consistent service standards.
  • Resolve guest concerns promptly.
  • Ensure every guest leaves satisfied.

1. Restaurant Opening Procedure

Before guests arrive, the restaurant must be fully prepared for service. Opening procedures should be completed at least 30–60 minutes before opening, depending on the restaurant's size and concept.

Opening Checklist

  • Unlock restaurant and conduct security inspection.
  • Switch on lights, music and air conditioning.
  • Inspect dining room cleanliness.
  • Check washrooms and replenish supplies.
  • Inspect tables, chairs and décor.
  • Prepare POS terminals.
  • Verify reservation book.
  • Ensure menus are clean and complete.
  • Confirm today's specials.
  • Conduct the daily team briefing.
Manager Tip

A restaurant should be completely guest-ready before opening time. Guests should never witness cleaning, table setup or unfinished preparation.

2. Telephone Etiquette SOP

Telephone conversations are often a guest's first interaction with the restaurant. Calls should be answered promptly, professionally and consistently.

Telephone Standards

  • Answer within three rings.
  • Smile while speaking.
  • Identify the restaurant immediately.
  • Introduce yourself.
  • Listen without interrupting.
  • Confirm reservation details.
  • Repeat important information.
  • Thank the caller before ending the conversation.
Recommended Greeting

"Good evening, thank you for calling [Restaurant Name]. This is [Your Name]. How may I assist you today?"

3. Reservation SOP

Every reservation must be recorded accurately to prevent overbooking, delays and guest dissatisfaction.

Reservation Information Required

  • Guest name
  • Mobile number
  • Email (if applicable)
  • Date
  • Time
  • Number of guests
  • Special dietary requests
  • Celebration details
  • Accessibility requirements
  • Preferred seating

Reservations should always be repeated back to the guest for confirmation.

4. Guest Arrival SOP

Every arriving guest should receive immediate acknowledgement, even during busy periods. Eye contact, a smile and a warm greeting help establish a welcoming atmosphere.

Arrival Procedure

  1. Smile and make eye contact.
  2. Welcome the guest within 30 seconds.
  3. Confirm reservation if applicable.
  4. Escort guests to their table.
  5. Present menus.
  6. Introduce the assigned server.
  7. Offer water promptly.
Service Standard

No guest should stand unattended at the entrance. If immediate seating is not possible, acknowledge the guest, explain the expected wait and offer comfortable waiting arrangements.

5. Seating SOP

Professional seating creates a positive dining experience and supports efficient restaurant operations.

  • Escort guests—never point toward a table.
  • Assist elderly guests and children.
  • Pull out chairs where appropriate.
  • Distribute menus neatly.
  • Ensure table settings are complete.
  • Inform the server immediately after seating.

6. Daily Pre-Shift Briefing SOP

A daily briefing aligns the team before service begins. It should be concise, informative and motivational.

Briefing Agenda

  • Expected reservations and covers.
  • VIP guests.
  • Menu changes.
  • Daily specials.
  • 86'd items.
  • Promotions.
  • Staff assignments.
  • Sales targets.
  • Guest feedback from previous service.
  • Health and safety reminders.
Best Practice

Daily briefings should last approximately 10–15 minutes and conclude with a motivational message. Encouraging teamwork, positive communication and guest-focused service helps create a consistent culture across every shift.

Restaurant Service Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs)

Professional restaurant service is more than simply taking orders and serving food. Every interaction contributes to the guest's perception of quality, value and hospitality. Consistent service standards ensure that every guest receives the same exceptional experience regardless of the day, shift or server.

Objectives of Restaurant Service SOPs
  • Deliver consistent guest experiences.
  • Improve communication between FOH and BOH.
  • Reduce service errors.
  • Increase guest satisfaction and loyalty.
  • Improve table turnover without rushing guests.
  • Increase average guest spending through professional upselling.
  • Maintain high hygiene and presentation standards.

7. Table Setup SOP

Every table should be prepared before guests arrive. Cleanliness, accuracy and presentation reflect the restaurant's professionalism.

Standard Table Setup Checklist

  • Clean and sanitise the table.
  • Inspect chairs for cleanliness and stability.
  • Lay tablecloths neatly where applicable.
  • Arrange cutlery according to the menu.
  • Polish glassware until spotless.
  • Place napkins correctly.
  • Check condiments and refill if necessary.
  • Ensure table numbers are visible.
  • Verify lighting and table décor.
Quality Standard

A guest should never be seated at a table that requires additional cleaning or resetting. Every table must be completely ready before seating begins.

8. Order Taking SOP

Order accuracy directly affects guest satisfaction, kitchen efficiency and food cost control. Every order should be confirmed before it is sent to the kitchen.

Order Taking Procedure

  1. Greet guests warmly.
  2. Introduce yourself by name.
  3. Explain daily specials.
  4. Recommend signature dishes.
  5. Ask about allergies and dietary requirements.
  6. Record every item accurately.
  7. Confirm cooking preferences.
  8. Repeat the order before submitting it.
  9. Transmit the order immediately through the POS system.

9. Food Service SOP

Food should always be served promptly, safely and professionally while maintaining presentation standards established by the restaurant.

Food Service Standards

  • Verify table number before leaving the kitchen.
  • Ensure the order is complete.
  • Carry plates safely.
  • Serve ladies and elderly guests first where appropriate.
  • Announce each dish while serving.
  • Check guest satisfaction within two minutes.
  • Remove unused service items discreetly.
Manager's Standard

Hot food should arrive hot, cold food should arrive cold, and every plate should match the restaurant's presentation standards before leaving the kitchen.

10. Beverage Service SOP

Beverage service should be efficient, knowledgeable and profitable. Team members should understand the menu and confidently recommend suitable beverages.

Beverage Service Guidelines

  • Present beverages within five minutes whenever possible.
  • Serve drinks using clean glassware.
  • Offer refills before glasses become empty.
  • Recommend beverage pairings.
  • Monitor responsible alcohol service.
  • Thank guests after each service interaction.

11. Upselling SOP

Upselling should focus on enhancing the guest experience rather than applying pressure. Recommendations should be relevant, genuine and beneficial to the guest.

Upselling Opportunities

  • Premium beverages.
  • Chef's specials.
  • Signature desserts.
  • Additional side dishes.
  • Coffee and after-dinner beverages.
  • Wine pairings.
  • Celebration packages.
Sales Principle

Recommend rather than sell. Guests appreciate knowledgeable suggestions that improve their dining experience.

12. Complaint Handling SOP

Complaints should be viewed as opportunities to recover guest confidence and strengthen loyalty. Every complaint deserves prompt attention and professional handling.

The L.E.A.R.N. Model

Step Action
Listen Allow the guest to explain without interruption.
Empathise Acknowledge the guest's concern sincerely.
Apologise Offer a genuine apology where appropriate.
Resolve Provide a practical solution quickly.
Notify Inform the manager and record the incident.

13. Bill Presentation SOP

The final stage of service influences the guest's lasting impression. Billing should be accurate, prompt and courteous.

Billing Procedure

  • Verify all items before printing.
  • Present the bill in a clean bill folder.
  • Answer billing questions confidently.
  • Process payment promptly.
  • Return change or receipts neatly.
  • Thank guests sincerely.
  • Invite them to return.

14. Guest Departure SOP

Every guest should leave feeling appreciated. A warm farewell encourages repeat visits and positive online reviews.

  • Open doors where appropriate.
  • Thank guests by name whenever possible.
  • Invite them back.
  • Assist with directions or transportation if required.
  • Maintain eye contact and smile.
Remember

Guests often remember the beginning and the end of their visit more vividly than the meal itself. A professional farewell leaves a lasting positive impression.

Kitchen & Back of House (BOH) Standard Operating Procedures

The kitchen is the operational heart of every restaurant. Regardless of how impressive the dining room appears, guest satisfaction ultimately depends on food quality, consistency, speed of service and food safety. A disciplined Back of House (BOH) operation ensures that every meal leaving the pass meets the restaurant's standards.

Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) within the kitchen reduce waste, improve productivity, strengthen food safety compliance and maintain consistency across every shift. Every member of the culinary team—from the Executive Chef to the Kitchen Steward—must understand and follow these procedures without exception.

Primary Objectives of BOH SOPs
  • Maintain food safety at all times.
  • Produce consistent food quality.
  • Reduce preparation errors.
  • Control food cost and waste.
  • Improve kitchen efficiency.
  • Maintain equipment correctly.
  • Ensure a safe working environment.
  • Support excellent guest satisfaction.

15. Receiving SOP

Every product entering the restaurant should be inspected before acceptance. Receiving staff play a vital role in protecting food quality and preventing financial loss.

Receiving Checklist

  • Verify supplier name and purchase order.
  • Inspect packaging for damage.
  • Check expiry dates.
  • Measure chilled food temperatures.
  • Inspect frozen food condition.
  • Reject spoiled or damaged products.
  • Confirm delivered quantities.
  • Record all discrepancies immediately.
  • Transfer products promptly to storage.
Best Practice

Never accept products simply because they appear acceptable. Every delivery should be inspected visually, physically and against the purchase specification before signing the supplier's invoice.

16. Food Storage SOP

Correct storage preserves food quality, extends shelf life and prevents contamination. Storage areas should remain clean, organised and monitored throughout the day.

Dry Store Standards

  • Temperature between 10°C and 21°C.
  • Store food at least 15 cm above the floor.
  • Maintain clear aisle space.
  • Label every product.
  • Rotate stock daily.
  • Keep chemicals separate from food.

Cold Storage Standards

Storage Area Recommended Temperature
Walk-in Chiller 1°C–4°C
Refrigerator Below 5°C
Freezer -18°C or Lower

17. FIFO & FEFO SOP

Restaurants should apply both FIFO (First In, First Out) and FEFO (First Expired, First Out) inventory principles to minimise spoilage and control food cost.

  • Label every delivery with the receiving date.
  • Move older stock to the front.
  • Place new deliveries behind existing stock.
  • Inspect expiry dates daily.
  • Discard expired products immediately.
Manager's Note

Poor stock rotation is one of the most common causes of unnecessary food waste and inventory loss.

18. Food Preparation SOP

Preparation should follow standard recipes and approved production schedules to ensure consistency.

Preparation Standards

  • Wash hands before handling food.
  • Wear clean uniform and hair restraint.
  • Sanitise preparation surfaces.
  • Use colour-coded chopping boards.
  • Use calibrated weighing scales.
  • Follow standard recipes.
  • Record batch preparation where required.
  • Return ingredients to refrigeration promptly.

19. Cooking SOP

Cooking procedures should ensure food safety while maintaining presentation and flavour.

Cooking Standards

  • Cook according to standard recipes.
  • Verify internal temperatures.
  • Avoid excessive holding times.
  • Check seasoning before service.
  • Maintain portion consistency.
  • Present every plate according to specification.

20. Food Holding SOP

Prepared food awaiting service must remain outside the temperature danger zone.

Food Type Recommended Holding Temperature
Hot Food Above 63°C
Cold Food Below 5°C

Holding food at incorrect temperatures increases the risk of bacterial growth and foodborne illness.

21. HACCP Monitoring SOP

Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points (HACCP) provides a preventive approach to food safety by identifying hazards before they become problems.

Critical Control Points

  • Receiving temperatures.
  • Cold storage temperatures.
  • Cooking temperatures.
  • Cooling procedures.
  • Hot holding temperatures.
  • Reheating temperatures.
  • Cleaning verification.
Golden Rule

If it is not monitored and documented, it cannot be verified. Temperature logs should be completed accurately throughout every shift.

22. Cleaning & Sanitation SOP

Cleaning removes visible dirt, while sanitising reduces harmful microorganisms to safe levels. Both processes are essential.

Cleaning Schedule

  • Clean as you go.
  • Sanitise food-contact surfaces regularly.
  • Deep clean equipment daily.
  • Wash floors using approved chemicals.
  • Empty waste bins frequently.
  • Clean refrigerators weekly.
  • Inspect grease traps.
  • Maintain cleaning records.

23. Personal Hygiene SOP

  • Wash hands for at least 20 seconds.
  • Keep fingernails short and clean.
  • No jewellery except plain wedding bands where permitted.
  • Wear clean uniforms daily.
  • Cover cuts with waterproof blue dressings.
  • Report illness immediately.
  • Use disposable gloves correctly.

24. Waste Management SOP

Effective waste management supports profitability and environmental responsibility.

Waste Reduction Practices

  • Monitor preparation waste.
  • Track plate waste.
  • Separate recyclable materials.
  • Measure food waste daily.
  • Review over-production trends.
  • Adjust purchasing forecasts.

25. Pest Control SOP

  • Inspect deliveries.
  • Keep doors closed.
  • Seal structural gaps.
  • Store food correctly.
  • Dispose of waste promptly.
  • Schedule professional pest inspections.
  • Maintain pest control records.

Bar Operations, Beverage Control & Financial Management SOPs

Food may generate the majority of restaurant revenue, but beverage operations often deliver the highest profit margins. Effective bar management requires strict inventory control, responsible service, accurate cash handling and disciplined purchasing procedures. Strong financial controls protect profitability while ensuring exceptional guest experiences.

Objectives of Beverage & Financial SOPs
  • Protect restaurant profitability.
  • Reduce beverage losses.
  • Prevent theft and fraud.
  • Maintain inventory accuracy.
  • Improve purchasing efficiency.
  • Ensure responsible alcohol service.
  • Strengthen supplier relationships.
  • Maintain accurate financial records.

26. Bar Opening SOP

Before opening, the bar should be fully prepared for service with all equipment operational, stock verified and workstations organised.

Opening Checklist

  • Inspect bar cleanliness.
  • Switch on refrigeration equipment.
  • Prepare ice bins.
  • Polish glassware.
  • Stock spirits, beer and wine.
  • Prepare garnishes.
  • Check draft beer systems.
  • Verify POS operation.
  • Count opening cash float.
  • Review daily promotions.

27. Beverage Service SOP

Professional beverage service enhances the guest experience and increases sales opportunities.

Service Standards

  • Confirm guest order before preparation.
  • Use correct glassware.
  • Measure spirits using approved measures.
  • Serve beverages at recommended temperatures.
  • Present beverages professionally.
  • Monitor responsible alcohol consumption.
  • Offer additional beverage recommendations.

28. Responsible Alcohol Service SOP

Restaurants have a legal and ethical responsibility to serve alcohol responsibly.

  • Verify legal drinking age where required.
  • Monitor guest behaviour.
  • Refuse service when necessary.
  • Offer water regularly.
  • Recommend food with alcoholic beverages.
  • Arrange safe transport when appropriate.
  • Notify the manager of any incidents.
Golden Rule

Responsible alcohol service protects guests, employees and the restaurant's reputation. Safety should always take priority over sales.

29. Beverage Inventory SOP

Accurate inventory management reduces losses and supports cost control.

Inventory Procedures

  • Count inventory daily for key items.
  • Complete weekly stocktakes.
  • Investigate inventory variances.
  • Rotate stock using FIFO.
  • Secure premium products.
  • Record all transfers.
  • Document breakages and spillages.

30. Cash Handling SOP

Cash handling procedures reduce financial risk and ensure accountability.

Cash Control Standards

  • Verify opening float.
  • Record every transaction through the POS.
  • Issue receipts for every sale.
  • No personal cash in workstations.
  • Conduct surprise cash counts.
  • Balance cash at end of shift.
  • Report discrepancies immediately.

31. Credit Card & Digital Payment SOP

  • Verify payment amount before processing.
  • Protect guest payment information.
  • Provide printed or digital receipts.
  • Report declined transactions professionally.
  • Follow PCI security requirements.
  • Never leave payment cards unattended.

Purchasing Process

  4. Obtain management approval.
  5. Issue purchase orders.
  6. Monitor supplier delivery performance.
  7. Review invoices before payment.

Emergency Procedures, Crisis Management & Business Continuity SOPs

Every restaurant should be prepared for emergencies before they occur. A well-documented emergency response plan protects guests, employees, company assets and the restaurant's reputation. Managers must ensure every team member understands emergency procedures through regular training and practice drills.

Objectives of Emergency SOPs
  • Protect guests and employees.
  • Minimise property damage.
  • Maintain business continuity.
  • Comply with legal requirements.
  • Reduce operational disruption.
  • Protect the restaurant's reputation.
  • Support effective crisis communication.

55. Fire Emergency SOP

Every employee should know the location of fire extinguishers, alarm systems, emergency exits and assembly points.

Fire Response Procedure

  1. Raise the alarm immediately.
  2. Notify the Manager on Duty.
  3. Call emergency services.
  4. Assist guests calmly.
  5. Use the appropriate fire extinguisher only if safe.
  6. Close doors where possible to slow fire spread.
  7. Evacuate using designated exits.
  8. Proceed to the assembly point.
  9. Do not re-enter until authorised.

56. Medical Emergency SOP

  • Remain calm.
  • Notify the Manager immediately.
  • Call emergency medical services.
  • Provide first aid if trained.
  • Protect guest privacy.
  • Record the incident.
  • Complete an accident report.

57. Food Recall SOP

If a supplier issues a food recall, affected products must be removed immediately.

Food Recall Procedure

  • Identify affected products.
  • Stop using the product immediately.
  • Isolate remaining stock.
  • Notify senior management.
  • Contact the supplier.
  • Record quantities removed.
  • Arrange safe disposal or return.
  • Complete recall documentation.
Critical Rule

Never continue using recalled food products under any circumstances.

58. Power Failure SOP

  • Remain calm.
  • Inform guests professionally.
  • Protect refrigerated products.
  • Keep refrigerator and freezer doors closed.
  • Use emergency lighting.
  • Contact maintenance.
  • Monitor food temperatures.
  • Record downtime.

59. Security Incident SOP

  • Notify the Manager on Duty.
  • Protect guests.
  • Contact security or police if required.
  • Preserve evidence.
  • Record witness details.
  • Complete an incident report.

60. Evacuation SOP

  • Follow evacuation routes.
  • Assist guests requiring help.
  • Do not use lifts.
  • Proceed to the assembly point.
  • Conduct head counts where appropriate.
  • Await emergency services.

61. Crisis Communication SOP

Clear, accurate and professional communication reduces confusion during emergencies.

  • Provide factual information only.
  • Avoid speculation.
  • Use approved spokespersons.
  • Maintain guest confidence.
  • Document all communications.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why are Standard Operating Procedures important in restaurants?

Restaurant SOPs ensure consistency, improve food safety, reduce operational errors, enhance guest satisfaction and help managers maintain quality standards across every shift.

How often should restaurant SOPs be reviewed?

Best practice is to review SOPs at least annually or whenever significant menu, equipment, legal or operational changes occur.

Who is responsible for implementing SOPs?

While senior management develops operational standards, every employee is responsible for following SOPs. Department managers monitor compliance and provide ongoing coaching.

How do SOPs improve profitability?

Well-designed SOPs reduce waste, improve labour productivity, minimise errors, strengthen inventory control, increase guest satisfaction and improve overall operational efficiency.

What is the difference between an SOP and a policy?

A policy explains what the organisation expects, while a Standard Operating Procedure explains exactly how employees perform the task.
