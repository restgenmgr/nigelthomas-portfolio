Espresso Drinks Compared: Same Beans, Different Balances

By Nigel A. Thomas, Hospitality Executive • Coffee & Beverage Series

Walk into any café and the menu looks long, yet most of it is built on one thing: a shot of espresso. What changes from cup to cup is the amount of steamed milk, the amount of foam, and whether chocolate is added. Understanding these ratios is the quickest way for service staff to explain the menu with confidence, and for guests to order what they actually want.

The Six Drinks at a Glance

Flat White1 : 2–3 (e.g. 30 ml + 60–90 ml)Thin, silky microfoamSmooth, coffee-forward
Caffè Latte1 : 4–5 (e.g. 30 ml + 120–150 ml)Creamy, light foamMild, milky
MochaEspresso, steamed milk and chocolate (e.g. 30–40 ml espresso, 150 ml milk, 20–30 g chocolate)Creamy, with foamChocolatey, sweet

Espresso: Pure Coffee, Nothing Else

A single shot of 25 to 40 ml, served with no milk. It is the base for everything else on the menu, so a well-pulled shot with a good crema matters more than any other skill at the machine. Expect an intense, concentrated flavour that some guests love and others find too strong on its own.

Cortado: Smooth Balance

Equal parts espresso and steamed milk, roughly 30 ml of each. The milk is only lightly textured, so the coffee stays in front while the sharp edge is softened. It suits guests who find espresso too strong but a latte too weak.

Cappuccino: Rich, Creamy, Classic

The classic one-third each of espresso, steamed milk and foam. The thick, airy foam is the signature, often finished with a dusting of cocoa. It is a smaller, stronger-tasting drink than a latte, which is the most common point of confusion at the table.

Flat White: Silky and Espresso Forward

About 30 ml of espresso with 60 to 90 ml of steamed milk and a thin layer of silky microfoam. There is no thick foam cap, so the coffee flavour comes through smoothly. Guests who like a cappuccino but want less froth usually enjoy it.

Caffè Latte: Light, Creamy, Comforting

The milkiest of the group: one shot of espresso with 120 to 150 ml of steamed milk and a light layer of foam. The taste is mild and comforting, which makes it the easiest entry point for guests new to coffee, and the best canvas for flavoured syrups.

Mocha: Coffee Meets Chocolate

Espresso, steamed milk and chocolate, often topped with foam or cream. It is sweet and dessert-like, and the chocolate quantity, around 20 to 30 g, decides how sweet the cup becomes. Many properties position it as a cafe dessert-drink alternative.

Service Tips for Staff

Explain drinks by ratio first, then by taste: “a flat white has less milk and less foam than a latte, so you taste more coffee.” Guests remember that. Keep measuring tools at the station so shot volume and milk quantity stay consistent from one barista to the next, because consistency is what guests notice.
