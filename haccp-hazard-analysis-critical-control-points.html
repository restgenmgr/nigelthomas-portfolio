A practical breakdown of the 7 principles that keep food safe — from receiving through to service.

HACCP: Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points Explained

HACCP — Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points — is the food safety system used in commercial kitchens worldwide. It's not paperwork for its own sake. It's a structured way of identifying where food can become unsafe, and building controls into the process before contamination happens, rather than catching it after a guest has already eaten the meal.

HACCP doesn't inspect food safety at the end. It builds food safety into every step.

The 7 Principles of HACCP

Every HACCP system, regardless of the kitchen or cuisine, is built on the same seven principles:

  • 1. Hazard Analysis — Identify biological, chemical, and physical hazards at every stage of the process.
  • 2. Critical Control Points (CCP) — Determine the exact points where a hazard can be prevented, eliminated, or reduced to a safe level.
  • 3. Critical Limits — Set the measurable limit (temperature, time, pH) that must be met at each CCP.
  • 4. Monitoring — Establish procedures to observe and record whether each CCP is under control.
  • 5. Corrective Actions — Define what happens the moment a critical limit is not met.
  • 6. Verification — Confirm the whole system is actually working as designed, not just on paper.
  • 7. Record-Keeping & Documentation — Keep evidence that the system was followed, for audits and traceability.

Where Critical Control Points Show Up in a Kitchen

In day-to-day operations, CCPs are rarely abstract — they map directly onto real moments in the kitchen:

  • Receiving: Checking delivery temperatures before accepting stock.
  • Storage: Fridge and freezer temperature logs, FIFO rotation.
  • Preparation: Preventing cross-contamination between raw and ready-to-eat items.
  • Cooking: Reaching and holding safe internal temperatures.
  • Cooling: Bringing hot food down through the danger zone quickly and safely.
  • Holding & Service: Keeping hot food hot and cold food cold until it reaches the guest.

Why It Matters Beyond Compliance

Food Safety is everyone's responsibility, hygiene is the foundation, temperature control protects quality, and documentation ensures continuity when teams change shifts, managers move on, or auditors walk in unannounced. A kitchen that treats HACCP as a living system — not a binder on a shelf — protects guests, protects the brand, and protects the license to operate.

Biological, chemical, and physical hazards don't wait for an audit. Neither should your controls.

Conclusion

HACCP works because it forces a kitchen to think ahead of the hazard, not behind it. Build the seven principles into training, into daily checklists, and into how every shift starts and ends — and food safety stops being a document you produce for auditors, and becomes simply how the kitchen runs.
