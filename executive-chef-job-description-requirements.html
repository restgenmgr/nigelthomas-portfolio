Executive Chef Job Description & General Requirements

By Nigel A. Thomas · July 2026

A ready-to-use job description template for hiring or applying to an Executive Chef role.

Role Summary

The Executive Chef holds full responsibility for a property's kitchen operations — from menu direction and food quality to cost control, food safety compliance, and team leadership. The role sits at the top of the kitchen brigade and reports directly to the General Manager or Director of F&B, translating the property's culinary vision into consistent, profitable, guest-ready execution every single day.

How to use this template: This is a general-purpose Executive Chef job description suitable for hotels, standalone restaurants, resorts, and multi-outlet properties. Adjust the experience range, cuisine focus, and reporting line to match your specific establishment before posting.

Job Facts at a Glance

PositionExecutive Chef
Reports ToGeneral Manager / Director of Food & Beverage
Direct ReportsSous Chefs, Chefs de Partie, Kitchen Stewarding
Experience RequiredTypically 10–18 years, including prior Sous Chef or Executive Sous Chef experience
EducationDiploma or degree in Culinary Arts / Hotel Management (preferred, not always mandatory)
CertificationsFood safety certification (HACCP / ServSafe or local equivalent) strongly preferred
Work PatternFull-time, shift-based, including weekends and holidays as per operational demand

Core Duties & Responsibilities

  • Kitchen leadership & team development — building, training, and mentoring a kitchen brigade capable of consistent, high-quality execution across all stations.
  • Menu planning & culinary direction — developing and refining menus that balance guest appeal, seasonality, food cost, and kitchen capability.
  • Food cost control & profitability — owning recipe costing, portion control, waste reduction, inventory accuracy, and supplier negotiations.
  • Food safety & hygiene compliance — enforcing HACCP-aligned standards, temperature control, allergen management, and regulatory compliance (e.g. FSSAI or local equivalent).
  • Staff scheduling & performance management — rostering, appraisals, discipline, and succession planning within the kitchen team.
  • Cross-departmental coordination — working closely with F&B service, purchasing, and engineering to ensure smooth day-to-day operations.
  • Quality control — maintaining consistency of taste, presentation, and portion size across every shift and every outlet.
  • Budgeting & financial reporting — managing kitchen P&L, labour cost, and food cost against targets set by ownership or management.

General Requirements & Qualifications

  • Proven track record running a kitchen of comparable size and cuisine style, in either a hotel or standalone restaurant setting.
  • Strong command of relevant cuisines (adjust to property needs — Continental, Indian, Pan-Asian, etc.).
  • Demonstrated ability to control food cost percentage against budgeted targets.
  • Working knowledge of HACCP principles and local food safety regulations.
  • Proven leadership and team-building skills, including experience training and developing junior chefs.
  • Comfortable working under pressure during high-volume service periods.
  • Basic financial literacy — able to read P&L statements and manage a departmental budget.
  • Physical stamina for extended periods of standing, lifting, and working in a hot kitchen environment.

Key Skills

  • Culinary technique across hot and cold kitchen, butchery, and pastry fundamentals.
  • Leadership & communication — the ability to lead a diverse brigade under pressure while maintaining morale.
  • Cost discipline — a instinct for where money leaks in a kitchen, and the systems to stop it.
  • Organisation — production planning, inventory management, and supplier coordination.
  • Adaptability — adjusting menus and operations to seasonality, supply chain shifts, and shifting guest preferences.
For hiring managers: The strongest Executive Chef candidates aren't just technically skilled — they can demonstrate a measurable impact on food cost, team retention, or guest satisfaction scores in their previous role. Ask for specific numbers during the interview, not just responsibilities.

Typical Career Path to Executive Chef

Most Executive Chefs progress through the kitchen brigade: Commis → Chef de Partie → Sous Chef → Executive Sous Chef → Executive Chef. Each stage builds the technical, financial, and leadership foundation the role demands. See the related career guides below for a breakdown of each stage.
