Boutique Resort Staff Hierarchy: Departments, Roles and Reporting Lines

Hospitality Operations Series • By Nigel A. Thomas

A small resort cannot afford a thick layer of management, but it still needs clear lines of authority. This page maps one resort manager, accounts and HR support, and six departments, using real job titles drawn from a live pre-opening recruitment list.

A note on the reporting lines. The recruitment list names the roles but not who reports to whom. The reporting order here is the standard hotel logic: supervisors lead their teams, and the kitchen follows the classic brigade ladder. Adjust it to match your own property.

How each department works

Culinary & stewarding

The kitchen runs on the brigade ladder: chef de partie, demi chef de partie, then commis I and commis II. Each step takes on more responsibility for a station. Stewarding sits alongside the kitchen under its own supervisor, because dishwashing, pot-wash and equipment hygiene are a separate discipline with their own standards.

Food & beverage

One supervisor directs the floor team of waiters, bartender and barista. In a boutique resort the supervisor is usually on the floor serving as well, which keeps standards visible and makes the team easier to coach.

Front office & guest services

The guest experience officer leads reservations, front desk, telephone and bell services, with the concierge handling local knowledge and special requests. The list calls for an Arabic-speaking reservations agent, a good reminder that language skills belong in the job description, not only in the interview.

Housekeeping & laundry

A housekeeping supervisor inspects rooms and allocates attendants, while the laundry valet keeps linen moving so room turnaround is never held up by supply.

Recreation & spa

The smallest guest-facing team has no formal lead. The fitness instructor and the spa and fitness receptionist report directly to the resort manager, or to a department head you add as the property grows.

Engineering

A single order-taker and administrator logs maintenance requests, tracks work orders and keeps the paperwork straight. For a small resort, technical repairs are often handled by an outside contractor, so this role is the link between guest complaints and fixes.

Making it work on a small property

  • Cross-train early. A bartender who can run the barista station, or a front desk agent who can answer the phone, saves a shift on quiet days.
  • Keep supervisors working. At this size, a supervisor who only supervises is a cost you will feel. They should be the strongest person on their team.
  • Give accounts and HR a clear remit. Payroll, purchasing approvals, contracts and staff files all land here, so keep their authority in writing.
  • Plan for growth. When a department gets busy, add a lead role before adding more staff, so reporting stays clear.
About the “Emiratis only” roles. The source recruitment list reserves the concierge, spa and fitness receptionist, and engineering order-taker roles for Emirati nationals. Rules like this vary by country, so check your local labour regulations before you copy the hiring conditions.
