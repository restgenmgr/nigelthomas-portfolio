Direct vs Third-Party Booking: What It Costs to Book a Guest Occasion

A guest calls ahead: it's their anniversary, and they want a sunset dinner arranged with a spa package to follow. That single request puts an Operations Manager at a fork in the road most guests never see — book it direct through the in-house team, or route it through a third-party booking platform. The guest experience can look identical either way. The economics, and who owns the relationship afterward, are not.

The moment is the same either way. Who profits from it — and who remembers the guest afterward — is not.

The Real Cost of the Third-Party Route

Third-party and OTA channels bring visibility and convenience, but that convenience is priced in. On a typical occasion booking, platform commission runs 15-25% off the top — before any coordination cost is even accounted for. The guest pays the same amount either way; the difference is how much of it the property actually keeps.

  • Revenue looks strong on paper, but net revenue after commission tells the real story
  • Guest contact details often stay with the platform, not the property
  • Any last-minute change goes through a third party before it reaches your team
  • Average booking value tends to be lower — occasion add-ons are harder to upsell through a platform interface

What Changes When It's Booked Direct

When the same occasion is booked in-house — concierge desk, direct phone line, or a returning guest's relationship with the team — the cost structure flips. There's still a cost to arranging it: staff time, coordination, communication. But it's a fraction of platform commission, and everything else about the transaction stays inside the property.

  • Full control over timing, personalisation, and recovery if plans change
  • Guest data retained for the next occasion — birthdays, anniversaries, return visits
  • Higher average booking value, because staff can upsell naturally in conversation
  • The guest's relationship is with your team, not a call centre

How to Shift More Occasions Direct

You're rarely choosing to abandon third-party channels altogether — they still bring in guests who wouldn't have found you otherwise. The goal is to make sure that once a guest is in-house or in conversation with your team, the next occasion they book goes direct.

  • Train front desk and concierge to always offer to handle the "next visit" booking personally
  • Capture guest occasion dates (anniversaries, birthdays) at check-in or during service, with consent
  • Make direct-booking contact details effortless to find — on the room key sleeve, the folio, the follow-up email
  • Reward repeat direct bookings with a small, genuine touch — not a discount that erodes the margin you just protected
A direct-booked memory becomes a direct-loyal guest.

Conclusion

Every guest occasion is a small decision point for the property, not just the guest. Third-party platforms have their place in the funnel — but the moments that matter most to a guest are exactly the moments worth bringing back in-house, where the margin, the data, and the relationship all stay with the team that earned them.
