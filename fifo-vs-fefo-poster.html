FIFO VS FEFO

RIGHT STOCK, RIGHT TIME, RIGHT QUALITY
Smart Inventory · Less Waste · Better Food Safety · More Profit
FIFOFirst In, First Out

Use the stock that was received first, regardless of expiry date (assuming expiry dates are similar).

Received 1 July → Received 5 July (Milk)
Use 1 July stock first — it arrived earlier.
Dry GroceriesPackagingCleaning Supplies BeveragesDisposablesCanned Goods Kitchen EquipmentNon-Perishables
  • Prevents old stock from sitting unused
  • Improves stock rotation discipline
  • Reduces storage congestion
  • Simplifies inventory management
  • Minimizes unnecessary purchases
FEFOFirst Expired, First Out

Use the stock with the earliest expiry date first — even if it was received later.

Received 1 July, Exp 15 July → Received 5 July, Exp 10 July (Yogurt)
Use the 5 July batch first — it expires sooner.
DairyMeat & PoultrySeafood Fresh ProduceEggsBakery SaladsReady-to-Eat Food
  • Any product with a short shelf life must follow FEFO — no exceptions.

Common Mistakes

  • Placing new stock in front of old stock
  • Ignoring expiry dates
  • Missing date labels
  • Mixing batches without identification
  • Keeping expired products in storage
  • Skipping regular inventory checks

Best Practices

  • Label every item with receiving and expiry date
  • Store new stock behind existing stock
  • Check expiry dates daily for perishables
  • Inspect fridges, freezers & dry stores regularly
  • Remove expired products immediately
  • Train staff on FIFO and FEFO; audit weekly
