Sales & Marketing in Hospitality: Complete Career Guide

The revenue engine of every hotel — every role, how the numbers actually work, and the real career ladder to Director of Sales & Marketing.

Nigel A. Thomas

Restaurant Manager • General Manager • Resort Manager • Hospitality Leadership Mentor

Connect on LinkedIn

Every other department in a hotel exists to deliver on a promise that Sales & Marketing made first. A banquet team can execute a flawless wedding, a kitchen can plate a perfect dinner, and a front desk can check in a guest in ninety seconds — but none of it happens unless someone sold the room, the event, or the stay in the first place. Sales & Marketing is the one department whose failure shows up immediately, in empty rooms and unbooked function space, long before it shows up anywhere else on the P&L.

This guide covers every role within hotel Sales & Marketing, how the department actually operates day to day, and the realistic career path from Sales Coordinator to Director of Sales & Marketing — and beyond, into the General Manager's chair.

What the Sales & Marketing Department Actually Does

Sales & Marketing is responsible for generating and securing business across every revenue stream a hotel offers: transient rooms, corporate accounts, group and MICE business, weddings and social events, and increasingly, direct digital bookings that reduce dependency on third-party platforms. Unlike most departments, which manage what happens once a guest arrives, Sales & Marketing's job is finished before the guest ever checks in.

Core Functions of Sales & Marketing

  • Corporate account management and contract negotiation
  • Group and MICE (meetings, incentives, conferences, exhibitions) sales
  • Wedding and social event sales
  • Digital marketing, website and OTA channel management
  • Brand positioning and public relations
  • Site inspections and client entertainment
  • Coordination with Revenue Management on pricing strategy
  • CRM and pipeline management

From the floor: Some of the sharpest operational instincts I've seen in hospitality came from Sales & Marketing leaders — because they're the only department forced to think several months ahead of the calendar at all times. A General Manager who has spent time in Sales understands demand forecasting in a way that operational-only backgrounds rarely develop.

Sales & Marketing vs Revenue Management

These two functions are often confused, and in smaller properties they may even sit with the same person, but they are fundamentally different disciplines. Sales & Marketing is relationship-driven and outward-facing — building accounts, closing group business, and shaping brand perception. Revenue Management is analytical and inward-facing — setting the pricing strategy, forecasting demand, and deciding which segment of business to accept or decline on any given date. The two must work in constant coordination: Sales can close a large group booking, but Revenue Management determines whether that group's rate actually maximises the property's total revenue for those dates.

Skills That Matter Most

  • Relationship building and account management
  • Negotiation and contract structuring
  • Reading demand patterns and booking pace
  • Confident public presentation and client entertainment
  • Digital marketing literacy — SEO, paid media, OTA optimisation
  • Resilience — sales is a target-driven role with real month-to-month pressure
"Every other department reacts to what's already been booked. Sales & Marketing is the only department whose job is to create the booking in the first place — which means their mistakes are invisible for months and their wins are the reason everyone else has work to do."

Career Progression: Sales Coordinator to Director of Sales & Marketing

Sales careers in hospitality tend to move faster than operational departments, because performance is directly measurable in revenue generated — a strong Sales Executive who consistently exceeds target can be promoted well ahead of the timelines seen in other departments.

Typical Progression Timeline

  • Sales Coordinator — entry level
  • Sales Executive — 1 to 3 years
  • Sales Manager (segment-specific) — 3 to 6 years
  • Director of Sales — 6 to 10 years
  • Director of Sales & Marketing — 8 to 14 years
  • Regional Director of Sales / General Manager — 14+ years

Director of Sales & Marketing is one of the most common launch points into General Manager roles, precisely because the position requires full P&L literacy, cross-department coordination, and direct accountability to ownership — the same demands the GM role makes.

Interview Questions for Sales & Marketing Roles

  1. Walk me through how you would build a new corporate account from scratch.
  2. How do you handle a client who wants a rate you know Revenue Management won't approve?
  3. Describe a deal you lost and what you learned from it.
  4. How do you prioritise your pipeline when you have more leads than time?
  5. How would you position this property against three direct competitors?
  6. What digital channels would you invest in first for a new property launch?

Frequently Asked Questions

1. Do I need a marketing degree to work in hotel sales?

No — a hospitality or business background is common, but the industry values a proven ability to build relationships and close business over any specific degree.

2. Is hotel sales a commission-based role?

Structures vary. Many properties offer a base salary with a performance incentive tied to revenue targets, rather than pure commission.

3. Can Sales & Marketing lead to a General Manager role?

Yes. Director of Sales & Marketing is one of the most established routes into General Manager, given the role's direct P&L accountability and ownership-facing responsibility.

Final Thoughts

Sales & Marketing is the department where hospitality meets pure commercial pressure — targets, pipelines, and quarterly numbers that don't forgive a slow month. For professionals who thrive on relationship-building and can handle being measured every single day, it remains one of the fastest and most direct paths into hotel leadership.
