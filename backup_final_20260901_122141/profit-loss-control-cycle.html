Profit is Not Lost at the End of the Month — It’s Lost Every Day

By Nigel A. Thomas • Hospitality Executive & Corporate Trainer

The Problem with Waiting for Month-End

In the hospitality industry, waiting for a monthly P&L report means discovering problems after they have already affected profitability. A 1% food cost creep over 10 days is a training issue; over 30 days, it's a budget crisis.

A 10-day P&L control cycle helps management identify operational gaps early and take corrective action before losses compound. The purpose is not to create more reports — the purpose is to create faster operational action.

How to Act on the 7 KPIs

  • Food Cost %: If >1.5% above target for 3 days, do a line-check on top-selling entrees.
  • Labor Cost %: If actual hours exceed scheduled by 4%, freeze overtime immediately.
  • Menu Mix: If a top-5 item drops in mix, re-train servers on suggestive selling.
  • MPLH: If it drops below baseline, analyze the "dead zone" and reduce labor during slow windows.
  • Waste & Variance: If variance >2% on protein, switch to FIFO audits at shift start.
  • Purchasing Price Variance: Flag invoices immediately — call suppliers for credit before paying.
  • Sales by Menu Item: If a signature dish drops below 5%, move it to the Chef's Feature board.
"Profit is controlled in cycles, not at month end. If you correct within 10 days, you recover margin before it compounds."

Make the 10-Day Cycle Your New Standard

Start your first 10-day cycle not on the 1st of the month, but tomorrow. Because profit lost today is twice as hard to recover next week. Track these numbers, act on them immediately, and protect your margins with speed — not just hindsight.
