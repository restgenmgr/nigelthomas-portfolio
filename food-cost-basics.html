Food Cost Basics: Control Cost, Improve Profit, Grow Business

By Nigel A. Thomas • Hospitality Executive & Corporate Trainer • CHS, ServSafe FPM

Why Food Cost Is the First Number Every Manager Should Know

Running a successful restaurant isn’t just about serving delicious food — it’s about understanding how much every dish actually costs. Many restaurants lose profit not because of low sales, but because they don’t monitor food cost properly. Knowing your food cost helps you set the right selling price, protect quality, and improve profitability.

The Food Cost Formula

Food Cost Per Portion = Total Ingredient Cost ÷ Total Number of Portions

Worked Example

Recipe: Veg Fried Rice

Ingredient cost = ₹120.00

Portions (yield) = 4 plates

₹30.00 / portion

Target Food Cost %

Quick Service (QSR): 25–30%

Casual Dining: 28–35%

Fine Dining: 30–38%

The right target depends on your menu, concept, labour costs, and business model.

Why Food Cost Matters

  • Helps set the right menu price
  • Improves restaurant profitability
  • Reduces unnecessary food waste
  • Maintains consistent portion sizes
  • Controls inventory effectively
  • Supports better purchasing decisions
  • Increases overall business efficiency

How to Reduce Food Cost

  • Standardize every recipe — use exact quantities every time
  • Follow proper portion control — serve consistent portions every time
  • Store ingredients correctly — prevent spoilage
  • Follow FIFO & FEFO — use old stock first, avoid expiry
  • Purchase from reliable suppliers at competitive prices
  • Monitor daily wastage and take corrective action
  • Maintain recipe costing sheets for every menu item
  • Review food cost reports every week

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • No recipe standardization
  • Over-portioning food
  • Poor inventory management
  • Expired stock due to improper rotation
  • Not recording kitchen wastage
  • Frequent menu price changes without costing
  • Purchasing without comparing supplier rates
Measure every ingredient. Control every cost. Grow every profit.
