General Manager Duties and Responsibilities

The Complete Hotel Leadership & Operations Career Guide for Hospitality Professionals

The position of Hotel General Manager is one of the most respected and demanding careers within the hospitality industry. A successful General Manager is far more than the person occupying the top office—they are the leader who brings together every department, every employee, and every guest experience into one seamless operation.

Having worked my way through the hospitality industry over more than three decades, progressing from restaurant operations into Restaurant Manager, General Manager and Resort Manager positions in India, I have learned that exceptional hotels are never built by one individual. They are built by motivated teams, disciplined operational systems and leaders who genuinely care about both their guests and their employees.

My international experience aboard leading cruise lines, together with exposure to hospitality operations in the Middle East and the United States, reinforced one important lesson: while every property is unique, the principles of excellent leadership, financial discipline and guest satisfaction remain the same throughout the world.

What Does a Hotel General Manager Do?

A Hotel General Manager is responsible for the overall success of a hotel, resort or hospitality business. Every department ultimately reports to the General Manager either directly or through Department Heads. While guests may only occasionally see the General Manager walking through the lobby, the decisions made behind the scenes influence every aspect of the guest experience.

The General Manager is responsible for delivering exceptional guest satisfaction while maintaining profitability, ensuring compliance with company standards, developing employees, protecting the owner's investment and creating a workplace where teams perform at their very best.

Unlike department managers who focus on one operational area, the General Manager must understand every aspect of hospitality operations including accommodation, food and beverage, housekeeping, engineering, maintenance, sales, marketing, finance, purchasing, human resources and health & safety.

The Hotel General Manager is responsible for:

  • Leading the Executive Committee.
  • Managing hotel profitability.
  • Increasing occupancy and revenue.
  • Maintaining brand standards.
  • Developing department managers.
  • Improving guest satisfaction scores.
  • Maintaining legal compliance.
  • Representing the hotel within the local community.
  • Working closely with hotel owners and corporate offices.
  • Creating a positive workplace culture.

Why This Role Is So Important

Many people believe that a General Manager spends most of the day sitting behind a desk reviewing reports. The reality is very different. Successful General Managers are constantly interacting with people—meeting guests in the lobby, reviewing operations with department heads, inspecting guest rooms, checking restaurant service standards, discussing budgets with finance, supporting recruitment, coaching managers and ensuring every department is working towards the same objectives.

Throughout my own career, I discovered that hospitality leadership is about visibility. Employees gain confidence when they regularly see their General Manager involved in daily operations. Guests appreciate leaders who are approachable, while department managers benefit from timely guidance and support. Leadership is not something that happens inside an office—it happens throughout the entire property.

"The best General Managers don't simply manage hotels—they inspire people, build strong teams and create memorable guest experiences that encourage visitors to return again and again."

Every decision made by the General Manager has a direct impact on the hotel's reputation, financial performance and long-term success. Whether it's introducing a new service standard, investing in staff training or responding effectively to guest feedback, strong leadership influences every result the property achieves.

Departments Managed by a Hotel General Manager

Although every hotel differs depending on its size and brand, the General Manager is ultimately accountable for the performance of every operational department.

Department Primary Responsibility
Front Office Guest arrivals, departures, reservations, concierge and reception services.
Housekeeping Guest room cleanliness, public areas, laundry operations and hygiene standards.
Food & Beverage Restaurants, bars, banquets, room service, kitchen operations and profitability.
Engineering Preventive maintenance, utilities, energy management and safety systems.
Sales & Marketing Corporate accounts, travel agents, events, weddings and revenue generation.
Finance Budgets, forecasting, payroll, purchasing and financial reporting.
Human Resources Recruitment, employee engagement, training and labour compliance.
Security Guest safety, emergency procedures and risk management.

One of the defining characteristics of outstanding General Managers is their ability to understand how these departments work together. When communication breaks down between departments, guest satisfaction declines. Strong General Managers encourage collaboration, accountability and shared goals across the entire organisation.

Leadership Responsibilities of a Hotel General Manager

Leadership is the foundation of every successful hotel. While impressive buildings, luxurious rooms and modern technology certainly contribute to guest satisfaction, it is the people working behind the scenes who truly create memorable experiences. The General Manager's most valuable responsibility is leading those people with integrity, consistency and vision.

Throughout my hospitality career, one lesson became clear very early: employees rarely leave companies—they leave poor leadership. Great General Managers create environments where employees feel respected, supported and motivated to perform at their best. When people enjoy coming to work, guests notice the difference immediately.

Effective hotel leadership requires balancing operational excellence with genuine care for employees. A General Manager must understand financial reports while also recognising the importance of celebrating team achievements, coaching future leaders and maintaining open communication throughout every department.

Key Leadership Responsibilities

  • Create a positive workplace culture.
  • Lead by example every day.
  • Develop future department leaders.
  • Conduct regular departmental meetings.
  • Support employee training programmes.
  • Promote teamwork across departments.
  • Encourage accountability and ownership.
  • Recognise outstanding employee performance.
  • Handle conflict professionally.
  • Maintain high ethical standards.

Leading Department Heads

A successful General Manager cannot personally supervise every employee. Instead, they build strong Department Heads who manage their own teams with confidence. Executive Chefs, Executive Housekeepers, Front Office Managers, Food & Beverage Managers, Chief Engineers and Human Resource Managers all rely upon the General Manager for guidance, support and strategic direction.

Daily communication with Department Heads helps identify operational challenges before they become larger problems. Morning briefings, executive meetings and departmental reviews ensure everyone understands occupancy forecasts, VIP arrivals, maintenance priorities, staffing requirements and revenue targets.

Rather than solving every issue personally, experienced General Managers empower their managers to make informed decisions. Delegation builds confidence, strengthens leadership throughout the hotel and allows the General Manager to focus on long-term planning and business growth.

Creating a High-Performance Culture

Culture is not created by posters displayed in staff corridors. It develops through consistent leadership behaviours demonstrated every day. Employees observe how leaders respond to challenges, treat colleagues and communicate with guests. Those behaviours quickly become part of the hotel's culture.

High-performing hotels share several common characteristics. Employees understand expectations, receive regular feedback, participate in ongoing training and feel recognised for their contributions. These organisations typically enjoy lower staff turnover, stronger guest satisfaction scores and improved financial performance.

Characteristics of Outstanding Hotel Leaders

  • Remain calm during busy periods.
  • Listen before making decisions.
  • Communicate clearly and consistently.
  • Support continuous learning.
  • Resolve problems fairly.
  • Focus on guest satisfaction.
  • Develop future leaders.
  • Maintain professionalism at all times.

Employee Recruitment and Retention

Recruiting talented hospitality professionals has become increasingly challenging across the world. General Managers play an important role in attracting, selecting and retaining quality employees. Successful recruitment involves more than simply filling vacancies—it requires identifying candidates whose attitudes and values align with the hotel's culture.

Retention begins on the employee's very first day. Comprehensive induction programmes, ongoing coaching, regular performance discussions and clear career development opportunities encourage employees to remain with the organisation. Investing in people often produces greater long-term returns than investing solely in physical assets.

Training and Professional Development

Continuous learning is essential within hospitality. Guest expectations evolve, technology changes and industry standards continue to improve. A General Manager must ensure every employee receives appropriate training to perform safely, confidently and professionally.

Training programmes commonly include customer service excellence, food safety, fire safety, emergency procedures, brand standards, leadership development, communication skills and operational procedures. Department Heads should also receive coaching in budgeting, scheduling, conflict resolution and team management.

"One of the greatest investments any General Manager can make is developing people. Buildings eventually require renovation, equipment becomes outdated, but skilled and motivated employees continue creating value every single day."

Performance Management

Monitoring employee performance should never be viewed as criticism. Instead, performance management provides opportunities for coaching, recognising achievements and helping employees reach their full potential. Effective General Managers establish measurable objectives and provide constructive feedback throughout the year rather than waiting for annual appraisals.

Performance indicators often include guest feedback, attendance, productivity, teamwork, service quality, hygiene standards, sales performance and compliance with company policies. Outstanding performance deserves recognition while underperformance requires timely coaching and practical support.

By creating a culture of accountability combined with encouragement, General Managers build stronger teams capable of consistently delivering exceptional guest experiences and achieving business objectives.

Financial Responsibilities of a Hotel General Manager

A successful hotel is built on two equally important foundations—exceptional guest satisfaction and sound financial management. While guests experience outstanding service, the General Manager must simultaneously ensure the business remains profitable, sustainable and capable of continued growth.

Every financial decision made throughout the hotel eventually influences the property's profitability. Labour costs, purchasing decisions, food costs, utility consumption, room pricing, maintenance planning and revenue strategies all contribute towards the hotel's overall financial performance.

General Managers are expected to understand financial reports just as thoroughly as they understand guest service. Numbers tell the story behind the operation and allow leaders to identify opportunities before they become problems.

Key Financial Responsibilities

  • Prepare annual operating budgets.
  • Monitor departmental expenditure.
  • Approve capital investment projects.
  • Control payroll expenses.
  • Manage purchasing procedures.
  • Review daily revenue reports.
  • Improve departmental profitability.
  • Reduce unnecessary operating costs.
  • Present financial reports to owners.
  • Develop long-term business strategies.

Understanding Profit & Loss (P&L)

One of the General Manager's most important documents is the monthly Profit and Loss Statement. This report summarises the hotel's financial performance by comparing total income against operating expenses. Department Heads rely upon the General Manager to interpret these reports and develop action plans whenever results fall below budget.

Rather than reacting to financial problems after they occur, experienced General Managers monitor trends throughout the month. Early intervention often prevents larger financial losses later in the financial year.

Financial Indicator Purpose
Total Revenue Total income generated from rooms, food & beverage, banquets and other services.
Operating Expenses Daily costs required to operate the hotel efficiently.
Gross Operating Profit (GOP) Measures operational profitability before management fees and fixed costs.
Net Profit The final financial result after all expenses have been deducted.

Revenue Management

Modern General Managers work closely with Revenue Managers to maximise occupancy while achieving the highest possible Average Daily Rate (ADR). Selling every room at discounted prices does not necessarily create the greatest profit. Successful revenue management balances occupancy with room pricing to achieve maximum revenue.

Daily discussions often focus on booking pace, competitor pricing, market demand, seasonal trends, group business, corporate accounts and online travel agency performance.

Revenue KPIs Every General Manager Monitors

  • Occupancy Percentage
  • Average Daily Rate (ADR)
  • Revenue Per Available Room (RevPAR)
  • Gross Operating Profit (GOP)
  • Guest Satisfaction Score (GSS)
  • Market Penetration Index (MPI)
  • Average Length of Stay
  • Cancellation Ratio

Budget Planning and Forecasting

Preparing an annual budget requires careful analysis of historical performance, market conditions, inflation, staffing requirements and projected business opportunities. Every department contributes information which is consolidated into one comprehensive operating budget.

Forecasting is equally important. Rather than relying solely upon the annual budget, General Managers regularly update revenue and expenditure forecasts to reflect changing market conditions. Accurate forecasting allows owners to make informed investment decisions while ensuring sufficient resources remain available throughout the year.

Payroll and Labour Cost Management

Payroll is often one of the largest operating expenses within any hotel. General Managers must balance service quality with efficient staffing levels. Excessive labour costs reduce profitability while insufficient staffing damages guest satisfaction and employee morale.

Labour scheduling should always reflect business demand. Occupancy forecasts, restaurant reservations, conferences, weddings and seasonal fluctuations all influence staffing requirements. Cross-training employees also provides flexibility while improving operational efficiency.

Food Cost and Beverage Cost Control

Food and Beverage operations require continuous financial monitoring. Purchasing procedures, supplier negotiations, inventory management, recipe costing and portion control all contribute towards profitability.

Experienced General Managers work closely with Executive Chefs and Food & Beverage Managers to review menu engineering, identify slow-moving inventory, minimise waste and improve gross profit without compromising quality.

"Profitability is never achieved by cutting standards. Sustainable success comes from controlling costs intelligently while consistently delivering excellent guest experiences."

Purchasing and Inventory Management

Effective purchasing systems protect both profitability and product quality. Competitive supplier quotations, approved vendor programmes, receiving procedures and regular inventory counts reduce waste while improving accountability.

Monthly stock audits, beverage inventories, linen counts, engineering spare parts and housekeeping supplies all require careful monitoring. Strong inventory controls reduce theft, improve forecasting accuracy and support healthier cash flow.

Owner Reporting

Hotel owners expect accurate, timely and transparent communication regarding business performance. The General Manager regularly prepares reports covering financial performance, capital expenditure, guest satisfaction, maintenance projects, staffing updates and future business opportunities.

Building trust with owners requires honesty, sound judgement and proactive planning. Successful General Managers present solutions—not simply problems—demonstrating their ability to lead the business through both opportunities and challenges.

A Typical Day in the Life of a Hotel General Manager

No two days are ever the same for a Hotel General Manager. One morning may begin with reviewing occupancy reports and preparing for VIP arrivals, while another may involve handling an unexpected maintenance emergency or meeting with corporate executives. The ability to adapt quickly while maintaining operational standards is one of the defining qualities of successful hospitality leaders.

Although every property differs, experienced General Managers follow a structured routine that allows them to remain informed about every aspect of the business.

Typical Daily Responsibilities

  • Review previous day's financial performance.
  • Attend Executive Committee morning briefing.
  • Inspect public areas and guest rooms.
  • Meet arriving VIP guests.
  • Review guest comments and online reviews.
  • Monitor departmental staffing levels.
  • Approve purchasing requests.
  • Review banquet and conference operations.
  • Meet suppliers and contractors.
  • Coach department managers.
  • Review safety and maintenance issues.
  • Plan future business strategies.

Weekly Responsibilities

Weekly management meetings provide an opportunity for Department Heads to review operational performance, discuss upcoming events and identify opportunities for improvement. General Managers encourage open communication and ensure every department understands current priorities.

Meeting Purpose
Executive Committee Meeting Review hotel performance and operational priorities.
Revenue Meeting Analyse occupancy, ADR and RevPAR trends.
Sales Meeting Review future business opportunities and corporate accounts.
Department Head Meeting Coordinate operational activities across the hotel.
Health & Safety Review Monitor compliance and identify operational risks.

Monthly Responsibilities

Every month the General Manager reviews detailed financial statements, payroll costs, guest satisfaction reports, marketing performance and maintenance schedules. These reviews ensure the hotel remains aligned with its annual business plan while identifying areas requiring corrective action.

Many hotels also conduct quality assurance audits and departmental inspections every month. These evaluations help maintain brand standards while supporting continuous improvement throughout the property.

Annual Planning

Long-term planning is one of the most important responsibilities entrusted to senior hotel leadership. Annual budgets, marketing strategies, capital expenditure plans and employee development programmes require careful preparation and regular review.

Successful General Managers work closely with owners, corporate offices and Department Heads to establish realistic objectives while identifying opportunities for future growth.

Annual Priorities

  • Prepare operating budgets.
  • Develop capital expenditure plans.
  • Review staffing structures.
  • Update emergency procedures.
  • Plan employee training programmes.
  • Review supplier agreements.
  • Evaluate business performance.
  • Set strategic objectives for the coming year.

Crisis Management

Hospitality leaders must be prepared to respond calmly and professionally during unexpected situations. Medical emergencies, power failures, fire alarms, severe weather, food safety incidents and security concerns all require immediate leadership and decisive action.

Preparation is the key to successful crisis management. Hotels should maintain clearly documented emergency procedures, conduct regular drills and ensure every employee understands their responsibilities during an emergency.

General Managers are responsible for coordinating communication between guests, employees, emergency services, owners and corporate leadership while ensuring guest safety remains the highest priority.

"Guests may never remember a perfectly ordinary day at a hotel, but they will always remember how professionally a team handled an unexpected situation. Strong leadership creates confidence during moments of uncertainty."

Decision-Making Under Pressure

Every General Manager faces situations where important decisions must be made quickly. Whether dealing with a major guest complaint, unexpected equipment failure or sudden staffing shortage, effective leaders gather accurate information, consult the appropriate managers and make decisions confidently.

Experience certainly improves judgement, but successful decision-making also depends upon preparation, communication and trust. Building capable management teams allows problems to be resolved efficiently while maintaining exceptional service standards.

Preparing for a General Manager Interview

Candidates applying for senior hotel leadership positions should expect competency-based interviews supported by operational and financial discussions. Recruiters want evidence of achievements rather than theoretical knowledge.

Prepare examples demonstrating how you have improved guest satisfaction, increased revenue, reduced costs, developed employees, solved operational problems and managed change successfully.

Interview Questions You Should Expect

  • Describe your leadership style.
  • How do you improve guest satisfaction?
  • Explain how you control labour costs.
  • Tell us about a difficult operational challenge.
  • How do you motivate department heads?
  • What KPIs do you review every day?
  • How do you increase hotel profitability?
  • Describe your experience with budgeting.
  • How do you manage conflict?
  • What makes an outstanding General Manager?

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the main responsibility of a Hotel General Manager?

The General Manager oversees every aspect of hotel operations including guest satisfaction, financial performance, employee development, legal compliance and long-term business strategy.

Does a General Manager supervise every department?

Yes. Department Heads manage their individual teams, but the General Manager remains accountable for the overall performance of every department.

What qualifications are required?

Most employers prefer a degree in Hotel Management or Hospitality Management together with extensive operational experience and proven leadership ability.

How many hours does a General Manager work?

Hospitality leadership is demanding. Many General Managers work well beyond the traditional working day, particularly during peak seasons, major events or operational emergencies.

Can someone become a General Manager without starting in management?

Absolutely. Many successful hospitality leaders—including myself—began in operational roles and gradually progressed through dedication, continuous learning and practical experience.

Which department provides the best route to becoming a General Manager?

There is no single route. Future General Managers have progressed from Food & Beverage, Front Office, Rooms Division, Finance, Sales and Operations. What matters most is broad operational knowledge and leadership ability.
