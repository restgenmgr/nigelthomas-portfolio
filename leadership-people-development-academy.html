Module 1 · Lesson 1

Behavioural Skills & Customer Experience

Behavioural training should describe actions people can observe and practise — not simply tell them to “have a good attitude.”

Communication

Use clear, respectful language; ask focused questions; confirm the next step; adapt tone to the channel and customer.

Active listening

Let the customer explain, avoid premature assumptions, summarise the issue and check that you understood correctly.

Empathy

Acknowledge the impact on the person without arguing, blaming or making promises you cannot keep.

Ownership

Clarify what you can do, take the agreed next action, record relevant details and follow through or make a clear handover.

Conflict & service recovery

Stay calm, acknowledge the concern, establish facts, explain realistic options and escalate when authority or safety requires it.

Customer-centricity

Balance customer needs with policy, fairness, safety and business limits. Explain constraints respectfully and offer viable alternatives.

A five-step service conversation

  1. Listen: let the person explain without interruption where practical.
  2. Acknowledge: show you understand the concern and its impact.
  3. Clarify: ask questions and repeat the issue in your own words.
  4. Agree: explain the action, owner and realistic timeframe.
  5. Follow through: complete the action, document it and check the outcome where appropriate.

Adapt this sequence to workplace policies. Never promise compensation, a deadline or an outcome outside your authority.

Practice 1: turn values into behaviours

Scenario: A resort guest says their room is not ready, they have waited 40 minutes and nobody has updated them. In a corporate chat scenario, a customer says they have repeated the same issue to two agents without resolution.

Write a response for one scenario. Include an acknowledgement, a clarifying question if needed, a specific next action and a realistic update commitment.

This tool checks for a few key signals. It does not use AI or judge the quality of your writing.
