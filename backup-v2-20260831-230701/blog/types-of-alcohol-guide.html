Types of Alcohol

From spirits to cider – a complete guide to understanding the beverage categories you'll serve every day.

Nigel Anthony Thomas
Hospitality Executive & Corporate Trainer • CHS, ServSafe Food Protection Manager • 30+ Years Across Hotels, Resorts & Cruise Lines

Whether you're running a bar, managing a hotel restaurant, or training new staff, knowing your alcohol categories is non-negotiable. In over thirty years of hospitality operations, I've seen too many service professionals stumble over the basics – confusing a liqueur with a spirit, or not knowing the ABV range of the wine they're pouring. This guide gives you the clear, practical overview every bartender, server, and manager should have at their fingertips.

"Drink responsibly. Know your limits. Serve responsibly. Respect your guests."

The complete alcohol family – spirits, wine, beer, liqueurs, cider, and traditional specialties.

1. Spirits (Distilled Alcohol)

Definition: Spirits are alcoholic beverages produced by distilling fermented grains, fruits, or sugarcane. They have the highest alcohol content, usually 35–50% ABV.

Common examples:

  • Vodka
  • Gin
  • Rum
  • Tequila
  • Whiskey
  • Brandy

2. Wine

Definition: Wine is made by fermenting grapes or other fruits without distillation. It typically contains 8–15% ABV.

Common examples:

  • Red Wine
  • White Wine
  • Rosé Wine
  • Sparkling Wine
  • Dessert Wine
  • Fortified Wine (Port, Sherry)

3. Beer

Definition: Beer is brewed from malted grains (usually barley), hops, yeast, and water. It generally contains 4–8% ABV.

Common examples:

  • Lager
  • Ale
  • Stout
  • Porter
  • Wheat Beer
  • IPA (India Pale Ale)

4. Liqueurs

Definition: Liqueurs are sweetened and flavored spirits made by adding sugar, herbs, fruits, spices, cream, or coffee to distilled spirit. They usually contain 15–40% ABV.

Common examples:

  • Baileys (Irish Cream)
  • Kahlúa (Coffee Liqueur)
  • Amaretto
  • Triple Sec (Orange Liqueur)
  • Sambuca

5. Cider

Definition: Cider is made by fermenting apple or pear juice. It usually contains 4–8% ABV.

Common examples:

  • Apple Cider
  • Pear Cider (Perry)

6. Traditional & Specialty Alcohol

Definition: Traditional and specialty alcoholic beverages are regional drinks made using local ingredients and traditional production methods. Their alcohol content varies by product.

Common examples:

  • Sake (Japan)
  • Soju (Korea)
  • Baijiu (China)
  • Mead (Honey Wine)
  • Cachaça (Brazil)
Golden Rule

Know your drink. Create memorable experiences. The best servers don't just pour – they educate and delight.

Why This Matters in Hospitality

Knowing the difference between a spirit and a liqueur, or the ABV range of wine vs. beer, isn't just trivia – it's the foundation of responsible service. It helps you recommend the right drink, pair with food, and understand guest preferences. In my training sessions, I always start with this taxonomy because everything else – cocktails, wine lists, bar inventory – builds on it.

When you can explain to a guest why a single malt whisky has a different character than a blended one, or why a dry rosé works better with summer dishes, you elevate the entire experience. That's what separates professionals from order-takers.
