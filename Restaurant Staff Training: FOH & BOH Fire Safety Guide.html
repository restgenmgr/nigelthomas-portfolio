Restaurant Staff Training: Front of House (FOH), Back of House (BOH) & Fire Fighting Safety Guide

In the hospitality industry, safety and service excellence go hand in hand. A well-trained restaurant team not only delivers exceptional guest experiences but also ensures workplace safety, especially in high-risk environments like kitchens and dining operations. This guide covers comprehensive training for both Front of House (FOH) and Back of House (BOH) staff, along with essential fire safety procedures and equipment usage for different types of fires.

1. Understanding FOH & BOH Responsibilities

Front of House (FOH)

FOH staff includes servers, hosts, bartenders, and managers who directly interact with guests. Their role is to ensure smooth service flow, guest satisfaction, and communication between kitchen and dining areas.

  • Greeting and seating guests professionally
  • Accurate order taking and communication
  • Maintaining cleanliness and ambiance
  • Handling guest complaints efficiently
  • Coordinating with BOH for timely service

Back of House (BOH)

BOH includes chefs, kitchen staff, stewards, and dishwashers. They are responsible for food preparation, hygiene, kitchen safety, and timely execution of orders.

  • Food preparation and cooking
  • Maintaining kitchen hygiene standards
  • Proper storage of raw materials
  • Following SOPs and recipes consistently
  • Ensuring equipment safety and cleanliness

2. Importance of Fire Safety in Restaurants

Restaurants are high-risk environments due to the use of open flames, electrical equipment, hot oil, and combustible materials. Fire safety training is critical for all employees, regardless of role.

Every staff member must know how to identify fire types and use the correct fire extinguishing method immediately to prevent escalation.

3. Types of Fire & Extinguishing Methods

Class A Fire (Wood, Paper, Cloth, Waste)

These fires involve ordinary combustible materials such as wood, paper, and fabric commonly found in dining and storage areas.

Best Extinguishing Methods:
  • Water-based fire extinguisher
  • Foam extinguisher
  • Fire blanket (for small fires)

Example: Dining room curtain catching fire or paper waste bin ignition.

Class B Fire (Oil & Grease Fires)

These fires are common in kitchens and involve cooking oils, fats, and grease. They are extremely dangerous if water is used.

Best Extinguishing Methods:
  • Wet Chemical Fire Extinguisher (Class K)
  • Fire blanket (smothering method)
  • CO₂ extinguisher (for small flare-ups)

IMPORTANT: NEVER use water on oil fires — it causes explosive flare-ups.

Electrical Fires

These fires originate from faulty wiring, kitchen equipment, or overloaded circuits.

Best Extinguishing Methods:
  • CO₂ Fire Extinguisher
  • Dry Powder Extinguisher

Safety Rule: Always switch off electricity supply before attempting extinguishing if safe to do so.

Wood & Solid Fuel Fires

Often found in grills, wood-fired ovens, or decorative wooden structures.

Best Extinguishing Methods:
  • Water extinguisher
  • Foam extinguisher
  • Fire blanket (small scale)

Paper Fires

Common in office areas, billing counters, and storage zones.

Best Extinguishing Methods:
  • Water extinguisher
  • Foam extinguisher

4. Fire Fighting Equipment Used in Restaurants

  • CO₂ Extinguisher: For electrical fires
  • Dry Powder Extinguisher: Multi-purpose use (electrical + flammable liquids)
  • Wet Chemical (Class K): For kitchen oil fires
  • Foam Extinguisher: For flammable liquids and solids
  • Fire Blanket: Used to smother small kitchen fires
  • Sprinkler System: Automatic fire suppression system
  • Fire Hose Reel: High-volume water supply for larger fires

5. FOH & BOH Fire Safety Training Procedures

Daily Safety Checks

  • Inspect fire extinguishers (pressure, expiry date)
  • Ensure emergency exits are clear
  • Check gas lines and electrical equipment
  • Verify kitchen hood and exhaust cleanliness

Staff Training Drills

  • Monthly fire evacuation drills
  • Hands-on extinguisher training
  • Role-based emergency assignments
  • Communication protocols during emergencies

Emergency Response Steps

  1. Raise alarm immediately
  2. Switch off gas/electricity if safe
  3. Use appropriate fire extinguisher
  4. Evacuate guests calmly
  5. Call emergency services

6. Conclusion

Effective restaurant operations depend on the coordination between FOH and BOH teams, supported by strong safety awareness and fire response training. Understanding fire types and using the correct extinguishing equipment can prevent minor incidents from becoming major disasters. Regular training, discipline, and preparedness are the foundation of a safe and successful hospitality business.
