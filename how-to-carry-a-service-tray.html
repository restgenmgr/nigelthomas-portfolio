Tray Handling for Food, Beverage & Bussing Service

PURPOSE — To ensure food and beverages are carried safely, professionally and efficiently while preventing spills, breakage and guest accidents. This applies to food trays, drinks/beverage trays and bussing trays alike, though each has its own balance points and handling rules, covered below.
1
The 7-Step Handling Sequence
1Prepare the Tray
  • Select the correct tray size for the load
  • Ensure the tray is clean, dry and free from damage
  • Use a tray liner or non-slip mat where available
  • Never overload the tray
2Arrange the Items
  • Place heavier items in the centre
  • Balance the weight evenly across the surface
  • Keep taller items near the centre, fragile items away from the edge
  • Confirm everything is stable before lifting
3Lift the Tray Correctly
  • Bend your knees, not your back
  • Lift using both hands and keep the tray balanced
  • Ask for assistance with large or heavy trays
4Carry the Tray
  • Carry on your open palm, or supported on your forearm at shoulder height for larger trays
  • Keep your wrist straight and stable; walk slowly and confidently
  • Keep your back straight, watch where you're walking
  • Never run while carrying a tray
5Moving Through the Restaurant
  • Keep a safe distance from guests and colleagues
  • Announce "Behind you" or "Coming through" in tight spaces
  • Avoid sudden turns; be cautious on wet or uneven floors
  • Use designated service routes whenever possible
6Serving From the Tray
  • Lower the tray onto a tray stand when available
  • Serve items one at a time while maintaining balance
  • Avoid reaching across guests
  • Rebalance the tray after each item is served
7After Service
  • Remove empty glasses and plates carefully
  • Stack items safely without overloading the tray
  • Return dirty items to the stewarding area promptly
  • Clean and store the tray properly after use
2
Not All Trays Are Handled the Same Way

Food, Drinks & Bussing Trays

???

Food Trays

  • Load plates by size — largest/heaviest at the back, closest to the body
  • Keep hot and cold items separated to avoid cross-contamination and heat transfer
  • Cover or shield plates in high-traffic areas
  • Never stack plates higher than the tray edge
  • Lower onto a tray stand before plating to the table
??

Drinks / Beverage Trays

  • Always hold glasses by the base or stem — never the rim
  • Group glasses by type; place tallest drinks in the centre
  • Use a bar tray with a non-slip surface for wet glasses
  • Carry lower and closer to the body for better control on turns
  • Wipe spills on the tray immediately — a wet tray is a dropped tray
??

Bussing Trays

  • Stack similar items together — plates with plates, glasses with glasses
  • Scrape food waste into a separate container before stacking, not table-side
  • Keep cutlery points-down in a cup or caddy, never loose on top
  • Never overload — bussing trays are heavier than they look once wet
  • Carry at hip height with both hands; never balance on one arm
3
Do's & Don'ts
? Do's
  • Keep the tray balanced at all times
  • Maintain good posture — back straight, knees soft
  • Walk carefully and at a steady pace
  • Use both hands when lifting
  • Ask for assistance with heavy trays
  • Stay aware of your surroundings
? Don'ts
  • Overload the tray
  • Run while carrying a tray
  • Carry an unstable or damaged tray
  • Hold glasses by the rim
  • Reach across guests to serve
  • Ignore spills or slippery floors
? Service Standard
  • Carry trays smoothly and confidently
  • Maintain a professional appearance and a smile
  • Protect guest safety and comfort at all times
  • Prevent spills, breakage and unnecessary noise
  • Deliver food and beverages efficiently
  • Maintain excellent service standards throughout

Good tray handling = safe guests + smooth service
+ a professional you.
