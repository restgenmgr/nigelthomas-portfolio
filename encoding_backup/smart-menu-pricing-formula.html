Menu Pricing: The Smart Formula for Profit & Value

Nigel A. Thomas August 2026 6 min read Menu, Pricing, Profit

Great food brings people to your restaurant, but smart pricing keeps your business growing. Getting your menu pricing right is one of the most important levers you have. Price too low, and you leave money on the table. Price too high, and you scare customers away. The sweet spot? Good pricing builds value. Smart pricing builds profit.

The Simple Pricing Formula

Every menu item should be priced using a clear, repeatable formula.

Menu Price (Selling Price) = Total Cost ÷ Target Food Cost %
Total Cost (per serving) Food Cost + Packaging + Wastage
Target Food Cost % 30% – 35% (industry standard)
Wastage Allowance 3% – 5% (covers spoilage & over-portioning)
Example — Biryani Plate
Food Cost (ingredients per serving)PKR 220
Packaging Cost (box, bag, sauce, tissue)PKR 30
Wastage Allowance (5%)PKR 12.50
Total CostPKR 262.50
Target Food Cost %35%
Recommended Menu PricePKR 749 – 759
Round off for easy pricing: PKR 750

Understand Your Costs

Before you can price anything, you need a crystal-clear view of what each dish actually costs you.

  • Food Cost — Cost of all ingredients used in one serving, measured precisely.
  • Packaging Cost — Boxes, bags, sauces, tissues, labels, and everything that goes out with the order.
  • Wastage Allowance — Covers spoilage, over-portioning, and kitchen waste (typically 3–5%).
  • Operating Costs — Rent, salaries, utilities, maintenance, marketing, software, etc.

Target Food Cost % by Restaurant Type

Premium / Specialty Cuisine35% – 40%

Tip: A lower % means higher profit potential, but the price must still match the customer's perceived value.

Smart Pricing Strategy

Pricing isn't just math — it's psychology and strategy. Here's how to price smart:

👤 Know Your Customer

Set prices based on their expectations and buying power. A customer who values quality will pay for it.

🔍 Research Competitors

Compare similar items and position your menu smartly — don't just copy, differentiate.

🧩 Use Strategic Pricing

Combo offers, value meals, and add-ons increase average order value without discounting core items.

💎 Focus on Value, Not Just Price

Great taste, quality, and service justify a good price. Compete on experience, not cost.

Best Practices

  • Calculate Regularly — Review recipe cost and menu prices every 30–60 days.
  • Track Your Numbers — Monitor food cost %, wastage %, and profit % religiously.
  • Adjust When Needed — Update prices when ingredient or operational costs increase.
  • Maintain Quality — Consistent quality builds trust and repeat customers.

💡 The bottom line: “Good food brings people to your restaurant, but smart pricing keeps your business growing. Price it right. Profit it bright.”
