Hotel & Restaurant Abbreviations: The Complete Guide

From ADR and RevPAR to F&B and MICE — master every essential abbreviation used in hotels, resorts, and restaurants.

Nigel Anthony Thomas
Hospitality Executive & Corporate Trainer • CHS, ServSafe Food Protection Manager • 30+ Years Across Hotels, Resorts & Cruise Lines

In three decades of hotel operations across India, the Middle East and the United States, I've seen too many properties fixate on one metric while ignoring the others. A hotel can have a great ADR and still bleed profit. It can have a high RevPAR and still miss the full picture of what's happening across the property. The real discipline is knowing which number to look at, when, and how they all fit together.

This guide is your definitive reference — covering not only the key performance metrics (ADR, RevPAR, GOPPAR, etc.) but also every other abbreviation you'll encounter in daily hotel and restaurant operations. Whether you're a hotelier, revenue manager, chef, or front-desk associate, this is the one resource you need to decode the alphabet soup of hospitality.

"A single metric tells you one thing. The relationship between them tells you everything."

1. Key Hotel Performance Metrics

These five metrics form the foundation of hotel performance measurement. Each one answers a different question about your business — from pricing power to operational efficiency.

Metric Full Form Definition Formula Why It Matters
ADR Average Daily Rate The average rate obtained for occupied rooms. Total Room Revenue ÷ Rooms Sold Indicates pricing performance and revenue potential.
Occupancy % Occupancy Percentage The percentage of rooms sold out of rooms available. Rooms Sold ÷ Rooms Available × 100 Shows how well the hotel is filling its available inventory.
RevPAR Revenue Per Available Room The revenue generated per available room. Total Room Revenue ÷ Rooms Available Combines occupancy and rate to measure overall room revenue performance.
GOPPAR Gross Operating Profit Per Available Room The gross operating profit generated per available room. Gross Operating Profit ÷ Rooms Available Shows profitability efficiency of operations.
TREVPAR Total Revenue Per Available Room Total revenue generated from all departments per available room. Total Revenue ÷ Rooms Available Measures total revenue productivity across all departments.

Each metric serves a specific purpose. ADR tells you about your pricing power. Occupancy tells you about demand. RevPAR tells you about room revenue efficiency. GOPPAR tells you about profit. And TREVPAR tells you about total revenue productivity across the entire property.

3. Worked Example: 100-Room Hotel

Let's walk through a full example using a 100-room hotel over a 30-day period (3,000 room nights available). This is the kind of calculation I run with every property I work with — it's simple arithmetic, but it reveals where the profit really sits.

📊 Revenue & Operating Data

Rooms Available3,000 Room Nights
Rooms Sold2,100 Room Nights
Total Room Revenue₹115,700
F&B Revenue₹7,000
Other Revenue (Laundry, Spa, Parking, etc.)₹1,250
Total Revenue ₹124,000

📉 Expenses & Profit

Departmental Expenses (Rooms + F&B + Others)₹115,000
Gross Operating Profit ₹9,000
Departmental expenses include all direct operating costs for Rooms, F&B and other revenue centres.

Metric Calculations

Using the formulas from the table above, here's how each metric is calculated from the example data:

₹55.10 ADR 115,700 ÷ 2,100
70% Occupancy 2,100 ÷ 3,000 × 100
₹38.57 RevPAR 115,700 ÷ 3,000
₹3.00 GOPPAR 9,000 ÷ 3,000
₹41.33 TREVPAR 124,000 ÷ 3,000

Notice that RevPAR (₹38.57) is not the same as ADR (₹55.10). The difference is occupancy. If this property could increase occupancy from 70% to 80% without dropping ADR, RevPAR would jump to ₹44.08 — a 14% improvement. That's the leverage point.

From the floor

I've worked with hotels where the team chased ADR aggressively and lost occupancy, only to watch RevPAR fall. I've also worked with properties that dropped rate to fill rooms and saw RevPAR improve — but GOPPAR didn't follow because the added volume came at a cost. The relationship between these numbers is always more revealing than any single one.

4. Why These Metrics Matter in Practice

In day-to-day hotel operations, these metrics serve different purposes at different levels of the organization:

  • ADR — The revenue manager's primary lever. It tells you whether your pricing strategy is working relative to your comp set.
  • Occupancy — The sales team's scorecard. It tells you whether demand generation is keeping pace with supply.
  • RevPAR — The general manager's dashboard. It tells you whether your room revenue engine is firing on all cylinders.
  • GOPPAR — The owner's bottom line. It tells you whether the property is actually profitable after all the noise.
  • TREVPAR — The operator's full picture. It tells you whether you're maximizing revenue across every department.
"RevPAR tells you how well you're selling rooms. GOPPAR tells you how well you're running a business."

5. Common Pitfalls to Avoid

  • Chasing ADR at the expense of occupancy — A higher rate with fewer rooms sold often lowers RevPAR and GOPPAR.
  • Filling rooms at any cost — Deep discounting can boost occupancy but destroy GOPPAR if the marginal cost of each room exceeds the marginal revenue.
  • Ignoring TREVPAR — Room revenue is important, but total revenue per available room tells you whether your F&B, spa and other departments are pulling their weight.
  • Looking at metrics in isolation — A high RevPAR with a low GOPPAR means you're spending too much to generate that revenue. The relationship is where the diagnosis lives.

7. Why These Abbreviations Matter

Understanding this "alphabet soup" is crucial for several reasons:

  • Efficiency: It allows for fast and clear communication in a fast-paced environment.
  • Professionalism: Knowing the lingo signals expertise and helps you fit in quickly.
  • Daily Operations: These terms are used in meetings, on reports, and in software systems every single day.

The Bottom Line

These five metrics — ADR, Occupancy, RevPAR, GOPPAR and TREVPAR — form the backbone of hotel performance measurement. None of them is perfect on its own. Together, they tell a complete story about pricing, demand, revenue efficiency, profitability and total productivity.

The properties that consistently outperform their comp set aren't the ones that obsess over a single number. They're the ones that understand how the numbers fit together, track them weekly (not just monthly), and act on what they reveal — adjusting pricing, managing costs, and optimizing the mix across departments.

"Numbers don't lie — but they do tell different stories depending on which one you're looking at. Learn to read them all."
